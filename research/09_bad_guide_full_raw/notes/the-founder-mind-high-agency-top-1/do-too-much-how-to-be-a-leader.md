# Do Too Much: How to be a Leader
**Source:** https://alexw.substack.com/p/do-too-much
**Author:** Alexandr Wang (Scale AI)
**Format:** Essay (Substack post, October 7, 2024)
**Section:** The Founder Mind (high-agency, top-1%)

---

## Faithful Digest

Wang's essay is a single, compressed argument: the correct amount of effort for a leader is "too much." It is structured around a series of reframings — things critics call excessive are just the correct calibration when you're building something real.

### The Core Argument

> "As a leader, you are the upper bound for how much anyone in your company will care. You need to do more, care more, attempt more than would seem reasonable. It will seem like overkill. But too much is the right amount."

The upper-bound principle: the founder/leader sets the ceiling for organizational caring, not a floor. The org cannot exceed the founder's level of investment. Therefore under-investing is multiplied downward; over-investing is the only direction that allows the org to reach adequate.

### The Reframings (verbatim)

> "What people say is overoptimism is just optimism."
> "What people say is overcommunicating is just communicating."
> "What people say is overdelivering is just delivering."
> "What people say is micromanagement is just management."
> "What people say is ruthless prioritization is just prioritization."

Each is a category error correction: the prefix "over-" signals a social norm violation, not an actual excess relative to the goal. The argument is that the social norm is calibrated wrong — calibrated to a median effort level that doesn't produce extraordinary results.

### The Scale Pivot Story

Wang uses Scale AI's response to the generative AI wave (2022) as the case:

> "Within 6 months Scale shifted the vast majority of our team to working on generating data for scaling LLMs. Most companies would go through quarters of bureaucratic planning cycles and only move after a competitor started eating their lunch. In our case, the change was drastic and abrupt — some might say jarring or extreme. What people might have reasonably described as overreacting was just reacting."

The conclusion: Scale's subsequent 4X growth was directly caused by what observers called overreaction.

### The Jobs/Musk Citations

> "There is no Apple without Jobs's 'obsessive' attention to detail. There is no SpaceX or Tesla without Elon's 'maniacal' drive for execution. I have never seen ordinary effort lead to extraordinary results."

The quotes around "obsessive" and "maniacal" are doing real work — these were critical labels applied by observers who had the wrong baseline.

### The Closing Principle

> "Creating something meaningful is a beautiful, and yes, scary and painful thing. And if you're not overdoing it, you're underdoing it."

This is the final reframe: the discomfort of "overdoing it" is not a warning signal. It is confirmation that you are at the correct effort level.

### What the essay is NOT saying

- It is not saying to be reckless or unfocused.
- The Scale pivot story is about *speed and decisiveness in a high-conviction direction* — not about flailing effort.
- "Too much" applies to caring, commitment, attention to what matters — not to scope creep.

## VERSO RELEVANCE

### [CONFIRMS] Founder-OS: the upper-bound principle
"You are the upper bound for how much anyone in your company will care."

For a solo/tiny-team project like Verso, this is most relevant in the open-source context. The level of rigor, taste, and commitment in Verso's public artifacts — the README, the Show HN post, the demo code, the invariant design — sets the ceiling for the community's investment in it. If the demo is mediocre, the community reads it as "this is how much the builder cares." If the demo-able wedge is exquisitely crafted (3-line side-by-side that is *actually* 3 lines and *actually* shows the delta), the community's engagement level will rise to match.

Tag: CONFIRMS founder_os. Verdict: confirm — already implicit, now explicit.

### [SHARPENS] The "over-" prefix as social-norm label, not calibration
"What people say is overoptimism is just optimism."

This is relevant to Verso's positioning claims. When Verso says "we don't force the graph" or "the agent loop is ~10 lines and not where anyone competes," these will read as overclaiming to some observers — especially LangChain-adjacent engineers who've invested in the framework model. The Wang reframe says: what they call overclaiming is just claiming. Verso should not hedge its positioning to be acceptable to the wrong peer group. The claims should be stated plainly and without the defensive softening that makes them look like "overstatement."

Tag: SHARPENS §12 (positioning / tone register). Verdict: sharpen — permission to state the counter-position flat-out, without hedging for framework-builder approval.

### [NEW-IDEA] "Overreacting is just reacting" — speed as the founding operating principle
"What people might have reasonably described as overreacting was just reacting."

The Scale pivot story (6 months, vast majority of team, before competitors moved) is a template for what the right response to a structural shift looks like. Verso is positioned in a structural shift: ~50M software engineers becoming AI engineers. The "overreaction" move is to build Verso now, build in public now, ship the Show HN now, before the market consolidates. The "reasonable" move is to wait until agent frameworks are a proven category with clear demand. Wang's framing says the reasonable move IS the mistake.

Applied operationally: Verso's launch timing should NOT be gated on readiness signals from the mainstream market. The overreaction (launching before the category is obvious) is the correct reaction.

Tag: NEW-IDEA §12 (GTM / launch timing). Verdict: adopt-now — reframes "launch before the market is obvious" as the correct move, not an aggressive gamble.

### [SHARPENS] Earnestness operationalized at scale
The Wang essay pairs tightly with the Graham "Earnestness" piece. Wang is showing what earnestness looks like in practice at organizational scale — it is the same direction (caring about the problem) with the magnitude turned all the way up. The Jobs/Musk examples show what sustained high-magnitude earnestness produces. For Verso: the intellectual honesty register ("decade not year," "explain it without AI") is the earnestness signal. Cranking up the magnitude of that honesty — more specific, more painful to admit, more willing to name what Verso doesn't do — makes the counter-position harder to dismiss.

Tag: SHARPENS §12 (positioning / POV register). Verdict: sharpen — combine with the Graham earnestness note to get the full picture.

### [CONFIRMS] The "compounded results" frame matches Verso's evaluation flywheel
"What we've accomplished to date represents the compounded results of everybody embracing the culture of overdoing."

The eval-corpus flywheel argument (Aggregation Theory applied to eval data) is exactly this compounding structure. Each agent run that feeds the harness evaluation corpus compounds the corpus's value. This doesn't happen at ordinary effort levels — it requires the overdoing culture that ensures every run is instrumented, every invariant is captured, every regression is caught. Ordinary frameworks don't build this corpus because ordinary effort doesn't compound it.

Tag: CONFIRMS §1 (bet/moat / eval-corpus flywheel). Verdict: confirm.
