## Core Thesis

> "I want to destroy the idea that launching is just one moment in time."

Launching is not a single event; it is a repeatable skill. Startups that wait six months for a perfect product before a "big bang" launch risk dying before discovering no one cares. The talk's organizing bet: **launch early and launch often**, using each launch to test a different hypothesis — your pitch, your audience, your channel, your product iteration.

## The Nine Launch Types

### 1. Silent Launch
**What:** Domain + company name + one-sentence pitch + contact info + CTA. Nothing else needed.
- No product required. The purpose is web presence and email list seeding.
- Ship by Product Hunt is the recommended no-code tool.
- Example: Zinc (Startup School company) used "get in touch" as its only CTA.

### 2. Friends & Family Launch
**What:** Private MVP testing with people who already know you.
- Do this as fast as possible after you have an MVP.
- Caveat: friends/family give filtered feedback; don't stay here long.
- Example: Reddit launched first within its YC batch before going public.

### 3. Launch to Strangers
**What:** Direct outreach to unknown potential users in real environments.
- Example — **Lugg**: Founders rented a truck, went to IKEA, identified customers struggling with cargo, pitched manually ("push a button, someone with a truck helps"), and validated the "hair-on-fire" problem before the app existed.
- Key insight: Real-world validation (strangers saying yes with their feet/money) > theoretical feedback.

### 4. Online Community Launch
**What:** Post in focused communities matching your target user.
- Channels: Startup School Forum (~40,000 founders), Bookface (YC internal, 4,000+ founders, "fairly low-risk"), Hacker News, Reddit, Elpha, Facebook groups, alumni networks.
- **Critical community norms:** Research each community's rules before posting; write conversationally, not "like a marketing robot"; share surprising insights; ask questions; connect with community members for guidance.
- Magic (personal assistant) got 40,000 signups overnight from Reddit/HN — Mañalac flags this as "an extreme case."

> "There is literally no reason that you shouldn't be launching to the Startup School community."

### 5. Request for Access / Waitlist Launch
**What:** Restrict access intentionally to create exclusivity and referral loops.
- Example — **Superhuman**: Users need referrals from existing members to skip the queue. Every email carries "Sent by Superhuman" — a viral tag baked into the core product experience. Access scarcity becomes a feature.
- Example — **Magic**: 40,000 signups overwhelmed capacity; they turned queue position into a social incentive (tweet to skip the line).

### 6. Social Media & Blogger Outreach
**What:** Identify relevant bloggers ranking for your target terms; pitch them organically.
- Example — **Joy** (wedding website builder): reached out to 50 top wedding bloggers; only 4 responded; those 4 drove significant early growth.
- Mañalac's rule: **Do not pay for blogger features early.** Paid promotion creates unhealthy patterns and depletes cash.

### 7. Pre-Order Launch
**What:** Collect payment/commitments before product ships.
- Example — **Sheertex** (unbreakable pantyhose): founder launched on Hacker News (predominantly male community — unexpected fit due to technical innovation resonance), Product Hunt, and personal networks simultaneously. Required multi-channel activation to hit the threshold.

### 8. New Product / Feature Launch
**What:** Established products should run the full launch sequence with every major release.
- Example — **Stripe Atlas**: Hacker News post (with founder participation in comments), blog post, social, press outreach — repeated with each major release. Founder participation in HN threads is non-negotiable.
- Example — **Glossier**: 6-8 week release cadence, hitting all channels every time (community, social, press, advertising). Creates a "constant drumbeat" that keeps the brand in constant market conversation.

### 9. Pre-Launch Community Building
**What:** Build your own email list during development to amplify future launches.
- Example — **Symmetria** (Gadi Evron): built an email list of every person he'd discussed the startup with casually. When TechCrunch published coverage, he emailed the list requesting shares. "The response was significant" — uninvested VCs shared from personal accounts.
- **Actionable tactic**: In every Startup School conversation, ask "Can I add you to my update list?" Build to hundreds before first public launch.

## Cross-Channel Data Point
> "Over time, we've looked at stats... they're starting to even out in terms of their impact and conversion."

TechCrunch, Product Hunt, and Hacker News historically had differentiated conversion profiles. Mañalac notes these are converging — meaning no single channel dominates; multi-channel is now table stakes.

---

## Key Warnings
1. **Don't wait for perfection.** Six months of building before launch = you haven't tested whether the problem is real.
2. **Don't pay for early promotion.** Organic blogger/community routes are harder but cleaner.
3. **Don't treat launch as theater.** Community authenticity (conversational, curious, founder-voiced) converts better than polished marketing copy.

## VERSO RELEVANCE

### [SHARPENS §12 GTM — cold-start Stage-1]
**Finding:** Mañalac's Stage-1 framework maps directly onto Verso's cold-start hypothesis. The Startup School Forum (~40,000 technical founders) and Hacker News are the exact channels where Verso's primary persona — structurally-aware senior/AI engineers — lives. The "nine launch types" reframes launch as an iterative epistemics exercise: you're not announcing a product, you're learning which audience segment is the "hair-on-fire" user.
- **Verdict:** Sharpen Verso's cold-start plan: the ~5 vocal production leads (atomic network) come from targeted community launches (Hacker News, Startup School Forum, AI/ML Discord servers), not press or broad social. Run the community launch before any press launch.

### [CONFIRMS §12 GTM — no-sales organic motion]
**Finding:** Mañalac makes "no paid promotion early" a hard rule, not a preference. Joy's example (50 blogger pitches, 4 responses, significant growth) shows the organic route has higher signal: the 4 who responded were genuinely interested, making them better amplifiers than paid placements.
- **Verdict:** Confirms Verso's DX-led organic GTM assumption. Do not buy sponsorships or newsletter ads in Phase 0. Quality > reach.

### [NEW-IDEA §12 GTM — "Sent by Superhuman" viral tag]
**Finding:** Superhuman's "Sent by Superhuman" signature is an in-product referral loop baked into the artifact the user produces. Applied to Verso: every trace, eval report, or inspect() output Verso generates could carry a lightweight referral signal (e.g., "Traced with Verso"). This is not a vanity banner — it makes Verso visible to the other engineers who review the output.
- **Verdict:** High-value NEW-IDEA: bake the referral loop into Verso's artifacts (traces, eval reports, session logs), not into the UI. Engineers share code artifacts; that's the propagation surface.

### [SHARPENS §12 GTM — multi-channel launch sequencing]
**Finding:** Stripe's approach (HN + blog + social + press with every release, founder active in comments) is directly replicable for Verso feature launches. The cadence matters: every non-trivial VERSO release (seam mechanic, eval API, inspect()) should trigger the full Stripe sequence, not just a tweet.
- **Verdict:** Sharpens Verso's launch operating procedure: sequence = (1) Hacker News with founder in thread, (2) blog post same day, (3) social, (4) email list. Repeat with every significant ship, not just initial launch.
