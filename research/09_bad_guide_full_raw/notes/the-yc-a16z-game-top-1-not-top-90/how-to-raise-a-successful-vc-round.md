## Full Digest

### Why Series A Differs Fundamentally from Seed

Elad opens with a structural observation: the VC market at Series A is an **oligopoly** — only a dozen or so firms that truly matter, versus hundreds of angels at seed. This oligopoly structure changes everything about negotiation, timing, and strategy.

VCs operate as professionals whose explicit goal is to "make money for their LPs." They have navigated countless similar negotiations. Series A terms are substantially more complex than a 3–5 page convertible note — they involve equity structures, board composition, and governance provisions that determine whether you remain CEO or get removed. These are long-term structural commitments with compounding consequences.

### The Seven Tactical Levers

**1. Compress Meeting Timeline**

Schedule all venture meetings within as short a timeframe as possible — a two-week concentrated window. The rationale:
- Generates simultaneous buzz across the small VC community
- Creates authentic auction dynamics and perceived momentum
- Prevents early rejections from spreading through the VC network before you've had all your meetings
- Builds founder confidence through parallel follow-up conversations
- Avoids premature term sheets that force difficult binary choices before you have full market data

**2. Engineer an Auction (Never Disclose Other VCs Until Term Sheet Signed)**

"VCs will call each other and collude." The dozen major firms maintain tight informal networks; revealing which firms you're also meeting causes them to pre-coordinate. Elad documents cases where revealing simultaneous conversations led to joint term sheets worse than individual ones.

The correct posture: allude only to "the usual brand name suspects" when asked. Decline syndicate offers explicitly — the math is: "Instead of selling 25% for $5M, sell 25% to each of us for $5M each" = 50% dilution for the same capital. That doubles your dilution.

Collect all term sheets within a few days of each other. Don't request term sheets too fast after initial meetings — rushing causes firms to exit the competition entirely.

**3. Run a Finalist Bake-Off**

After all term sheets: select 2–3 most interested VCs for intensive negotiation. **Maintain multiple options as your BATNA throughout.** Even if one firm strongly attracts you, keep others live as negotiating leverage.

**4. Use Practice Meetings First**

Identify venture partners you'd never accept funding from. Schedule them first. They provide free pitch feedback before you approach your top-tier targets. This is basically A/B testing your pitch against non-consequential audiences.

**5. Target Specific Partners at Specific Firms (Not Firm-Level)**

Obtain introductions to partners with relevant portfolio track records — not generic firm intros. The critical mechanic: **the first partner you meet typically "owns" the deal**. They negotiate your term sheet and take the board seat based on carry incentives that reward board participation. This happens "even when other partners might better serve your company strategically or operationally."

The instruction: "you will be stuck with the first partner you meet as a board member most of the time." So who you meet first is a permanent structural decision.

Vet the introducer's motivation — if they're introducing you because they owe the VC a favor ("currying favor or trading deals"), you're not getting a strategic introduction, you're being used as relationship currency. Ask the introducer to justify their partner recommendation.

**6. Deploy Backchannel Strategically**

Ask existing angels and advisors to advocate for you before VC meetings — informal diplomatic channels. After meetings, angels can amplify deal excitement and communicate competitive term positions across the community. This is pre-summit negotiation.

**7. Know Your Optimization Targets in Advance**

Define before you start: what matters most — valuation, control structures, expertise access, or board composition? Maintain a mental checklist. "Don't lose sight of these fundamental objectives during weeks of intensive negotiation." The process itself will generate pressure to optimize for the wrong thing in the moment.

---

### Pitch Mechanics

- Make pitches conversational, not slide-by-slide deliveries. Reference slides as supporting material, not the primary artifact.
- Introduce your team upfront to establish credibility before the idea.
- Prepare answers to: "which 2–3 key hires does your company need immediately?"
