## Faithful Digest

Matuschak's concept is deceptively simple and structurally important: **working with the garage door up** means sharing your creative process openly — not finished work announcements, but the messy, iterative, half-resolved work of creation as it happens.

### What it looks like in practice

- Streaming work-in-progress on Twitch (the origin context: game developers)
- Discussing unresolved problems publicly
- Documenting creative choices in real time (color palettes, architecture decisions)
- Revealing where projects fail — not just where they succeed
- "I want to see the process. I want to see you trim the artichoke."

The artichoke metaphor is load-bearing: it's not the finished dish that builds investment, it's watching the messy preparation — the trimming, the decisions, the discards.

### Two structural benefits Matuschak identifies

**1. Building invested communities**
This transparency attracts more meaningful, engaged followings than traditional announcements. The audience has skin in the game — they've been watching you work, so they care about the outcome. This is qualitatively different from a launched-product audience.

**2. Avoiding "pitching out corrupts within"**
By continuously showing work without pitching, creators sidestep the corrupting influence of performative promotion. You're demonstrating, not promoting. The audience self-selects for people who value the substance, not the pitch.

### The unexpected opportunity mechanism

Maggie Appleton's observation (cited by Matuschak): "Learning publicly creates a beneficial perception: people will assume you're more competent than you are. This will get you invites to very cool exclusive events."

The mechanism: visible process work signals effort, depth, and seriousness even before the output is polished. The audience makes inferences about competence from the *quality of the questions you're wrestling with*, not just the quality of your answers.

### The Robin Sloan contrast (the physical analogy)

Robin Sloan's original inspiration is a physical metaphor: an open workshop door signals "I am here, working." On social media, visibility requires constant communication. The selection bias created by silence: all the quiet, substantial work happening everywhere becomes invisible to potential collaborators. Opening the door creates the surface area for collaboration.

### What it is NOT

- It's not a marketing tactic or a "launch strategy"
- It's not polished "behind the scenes" content
- It's not a show of competence — it can include failures
- It does not require finished work; in fact, finished work announcements are *not* "garage door up"

### The contrast with "launch post" culture

Traditional product marketing: hide until ready, then announce with maximum polish. "Garage door up" inverts this: the audience is already invested before the announcement, and the announcement is an outcome they share, not a pitch they receive.

## VERSO RELEVANCE

### [NEW-IDEA] §12 — GTM/X: Garage-door-up as the *mechanism* of "build in public" — not a synonym for it

**Finding:** There's an important distinction this source surfaces that prior passes have not made explicit: "build in public" is often interpreted as posting polished launch updates and milestone announcements. That is *not* garage-door-up. Garage-door-up for Verso means: sharing the architectural decision in the moment of uncertainty ("I'm deciding whether to model the seam as a flag or a context manager — here's why both feel wrong"), the failed eval run with the actual numbers, the open question about when to surface HITL vs. handle automatically. These are qualitatively different posts from "Verso now supports checkpoint/resume." The former builds invested collaborators; the latter builds followers.

**Verdict:** adopt-now — explicitly distinguish "milestone announcement" from "garage-door-up process sharing" in Verso's X content strategy. Ratio should heavily favor process.

### [SHARPENS] §12 — GTM/X: "Pitching out corrupts within" as the failure mode for Verso's X account

**Finding:** If Verso's X account becomes a product promotion channel (feature releases, benchmark numbers, launch announcements), it becomes a pitch channel — and the audience that follows a pitch channel is a different, shallower audience than the one that follows a process channel. Matuschak's warning is that the pitch posture corrupts the authenticity of the signal. For Verso, this means: avoid making X primarily a release-announcement channel. The eval-run failures, the architecture debates, the "I rejected this design because..." posts are the garage-door-up content.

**Verdict:** adopt-now — define a content policy for Verso's X account that caps promotional/announcement posts at <20% of total posts. Majority must be process/problem/decision content.

### [SHARPENS] §7 — Demo-able wedge / product taste: The "artichoke" model for demo design

**Finding:** Matuschak's "trim the artichoke" framing is directly applicable to how Verso demos the one-flag durable diff and the inspect() surface. The current demo concept (3-line side-by-side, one flag) is a polished *result*. A garage-door-up version of the demo would show: "here's what I tried first and why it was wrong, here's where I got stuck, here's the question that unlocked the design." This is higher-resonance with the structurally-aware senior engineer persona — they've been at that exact stuck point and they recognize it. Recognition of the struggle is the hook; the solution is the payoff.

**Verdict:** sharpen — add a "what I tried first and why it failed" narrative to the demo script; don't open with the clean result.

### [NEW-IDEA] founder_os — Matuschak's invested-community mechanism vs. follower accumulation

**Finding:** Matuschak's structural claim is that garage-door-up creates *invested* audiences who share your outcome as their own, vs. follower accumulation that creates audiences who observe your outcome from a distance. For Verso, the goal is the former: the ~500-person inner circle of AI engineers who feel like they helped figure out the right eval architecture because they watched the process. These are the people who will be the superconsumer first adopters, who will post "I've been watching this since the open questions phase, and the implementation is exactly right." That's inbound recruiting, not marketing.

**Verdict:** adopt-now — frame the goal of Verso's pre-launch public presence as building an invested cohort of ~500 structurally-aware AI engineers, not maximizing follower count.

### [CONFIRMS] §12 — GTM: "Open questions" format is the right altitude for X posts (prior pass)

**Finding:** Matuschak's concept confirms the prior-pass insight about showing half-finished process. The garage-door-up framing adds specificity: the specific format is sharing open questions (not settled answers), architectural decisions in-flight (not announced features), and real failure data (not curated benchmark wins).

**Verdict:** confirm — reinforces the "open questions" format as the correct altitude for Verso's build-in-public X presence.
