## FAITHFUL DIGEST

Packy McCormick's "Most Human Wins" is his 2025 thesis essay — a deliberately slow-cooked piece (multiple drafts, 8-9 versions, months in development) about how to position yourself as a human creator or founder in the era when AI commoditizes most cognitive work. The core argument is structured through two lenses: the meditation philosophy of *neti neti* and a historical case study of Einstein and Marcel Grossmann.

### The Neti Neti Framework

*Neti neti* is a Sanskrit meditation concept: "not this, not this" — the practice of negation to identify what remains when everything that can be stripped away is stripped away. In meditation, the point is to observe thoughts and emotions coming and going to reach the insight: "I am not my thoughts, because I can observe them come and go. I am not my emotions, because I can watch them rise and fall."

McCormick extends this to economics:

> "I am not a farmer, because I can go to the grocery store."  
> "I am not a heavy object carrier, because I can drive a truck."  
> "I am not a computer, because I can use a computer."

**The principle:** As technology commoditizes formerly rare skills, it *clarifies* what is uniquely human by stripping away the layers that can be offloaded. Commoditization is not an attack on human value — it is a revelation of human value. Each wave of automation reveals the next layer up that remains genuinely human.

This is not denial or cope. It is a structural claim: the layer that AI cannot yet commoditize is the layer that becomes scarce and therefore valuable.

### The Einstein-Grossmann Case Study

McCormick's central example is detailed:

- In **1907**, Einstein had an intuition about gravity (the equivalence principle — a person in a falling elevator feels no gravity) but lacked the mathematical language to formalize it.
- In **1912**, he reconnected with his ETH Zurich classmate Marcel Grossmann, a mathematician who specialized in Riemannian geometry and tensor calculus — a highly abstract branch of mathematics that existed purely as pure math with no known physical applications.
- Grossmann recognized that Riemannian geometry could be the formal language Einstein needed. Together they produced the "Entwurf" paper (1912-1913), approaching General Relativity.
- Einstein spent two more years refining the equations alone, finally arriving at the correct field equations and presenting them to the Prussian Academy in November 1915.

McCormick's conclusion: **"Einstein commoditized Grossmann, in a way. He used Grossmann's output as his input, while he himself provided the spark that could not be reduced, the intuition."**

The modern translation: McCormick asked o1 Pro to solve advanced tensor calculus. It returned solutions in a couple of minutes. The cost: $200/month subscription. "Marcel Grossmann, included."

### The Asking Questions Thesis

The insight that follows:

> "The cheaper Grossmann becomes, the more valuable Einstein becomes."

As analytical, mathematical, and execution capacity becomes cheaper (AI), the **ability to ask the right question becomes the scarce and valuable skill**. Einstein's contribution was not the math — it was the *intuition* that gravity was equivalent to acceleration, and the *judgment* to recognize that existing pure mathematics (Riemannian geometry) was the right tool when Grossmann suggested it.

McCormick identifies this as the 2025 positioning brief: stop competing on execution capacity (which AI is commoditizing) and invest in intuition, taste, judgment, and the ability to identify the right problems. The economic endgame, in his framing, is "maximizing novel human experience, relations, and love" — things that are irreducible.

### The "Good Slop" Problem and Creative Differentiation

McCormick is unusually candid about the threat to his own work:

> "Even pretty good writing is commoditized."

He explicitly avoided producing what he calls "slop, even good slop" — polished, AI-assisted content that hits the surface markers of quality but contains no irreducible human insight. His 2025 changes:

- Three presenting sponsors (Rox, Ramp, Vanta) without fixed publishing dates — deadlines create slop
- Essays publish only when genuinely ready
- 45+ consecutive days of meditation (non-headphones commute walks for original thinking)
- Removed deadline pressure to give ideas "room to breathe"
- Multiple drafts (8-9 versions) with AI (Claude, ChatGPT) as feedback tool, not as generator

The meta-point: his writing *process* is the demonstration of the thesis. He is not just arguing that humans should invest in irreducible qualities — he is restructuring his editorial operation around that belief.

### The Cross-Pollination Source

One detail McCormick mentions: the unexpected source material that cracked open his thinking was reading *There Is No Antimemetics Division* (a sci-fi novella about ideas that resist being remembered) alongside CIA Reading Room materials. This juxtaposition produced a conceptual synthesis he couldn't have reached by reading "relevant" content. The implication: **novel insight comes from unexpected cross-domain input, not from consuming more of the same domain**.

### The Closing Argument

> "We want to live longer and travel further and accumulate more resources in order to experience more of what it means to be human."

The ultimate human advantage is not productivity or output — it is the capacity for *novel experience*. AI is an instrument for amplifying human experience, not a replacement for it. "Make them lift you up."

Practical strategy: Identify what's being commoditized in your field, use that commoditized layer as leverage (as Einstein used Grossmann), and focus work on the genuinely unique contribution only you can make this year.

## VERSO RELEVANCE

### [SHARPENS] §12 Positioning — The "wrapper" anti-pattern and the product taste moat

McCormick's "good slop" problem is the exact failure mode Verso's positioning must avoid. A framework that is merely "better than LangChain" is good slop — it hits the surface markers (better DX, fewer abstractions, cleaner API) but contains no irreducible insight. The Verso bet (harness-is-the-moat, one-flag durable diff, context engineering) must be the Einstein layer, not the Grossmann layer.

The already-established counter-position ("we don't force the graph") gets sharper through this lens: the graph abstraction is the Grossmann layer — useful execution scaffolding that AI (or simpler tools) will commoditize. The judgment about *when* to promote an ephemeral agent to durable, *what invariants to enforce*, *which context to preserve* — that is the Einstein layer, irreducible to a template.

**Verdict: sharpen** — every positioning statement should be stress-tested against "is this Grossmann (execution, commoditizable) or Einstein (judgment, irreducible)?" If it's Grossmann, it's table stakes, not a moat.

### [NEW-IDEA] §12 Positioning — "Neti neti" as the Verso category-design posture

The neti neti framework provides a structurally elegant counter-positioning argument: "I am not a framework, because I can use a framework. I am not a graph-builder, because I can use a graph. I am not an orchestrator, because I can call the API directly." Verso strips away the layers that can be offloaded until what remains is the irreducible harness — the context engineering, the eval loop, the checkpoint/resume seam.

This is a specific, memorable way to describe Verso's non-goals in public: not as a refusal ("we don't do X") but as a revelation through negation ("the harness is what remains when you strip away everything a better tool already does"). The neti neti framing is also resonant with the "explain it without AI" intellectual honesty register — it invites the reader into a shared process of elimination.

**Verdict: adopt-now** — test "neti neti" framing in X posts/Show HN as a positioning move. "What is a harness? It's what's left when you strip away the framework, the runtime, and the orchestration graph."

### [SHARPENS] §1 Moat — Taste and judgment are irreducible to commoditization

McCormick's essay confirms that in an era of AI-commoditized execution, **taste and judgment are the non-replicable moat**. For Verso, this maps precisely: the harness's invariants (what Verso enforces by default, what it refuses to do, what the one-flag durable diff reveals about context engineering) are taste decisions. They cannot be reverse-engineered from an API. They require someone who has shipped production agents and learned from failure what the actual decision surfaces are.

Prior passes established "invariants-as-taste." McCormick's framework sharpens why this is a moat and not just an aesthetic choice: invariants are the Einstein layer. A competitor can copy the API; they cannot copy the judgment about which invariants matter.

**Verdict: sharpen** — in positioning material, frame Verso's invariants explicitly as taste decisions made by someone with production experience. Not "our framework enforces X" but "we decided X was non-negotiable after seeing Y fail in production."

### [SHARPENS] §12 GTM — The "add one unexpected source" operating principle

McCormick's creative breakthrough came from *There Is No Antimemetics Division* + CIA Reading Room, not from reading more AI essays. This sharpens the Verso build-in-public post strategy: the most interesting technical posts will not come from consuming more agent framework content — they will come from applying an unexpected conceptual lens (information theory, operating systems, event sourcing, biology) to the agent loop.

This is consistent with the "OS analogy" and "march-of-nines" insights already established — both came from importing frameworks from adjacent domains. The GTM implication: deliberately seek non-obvious cross-domain inputs and show the synthesis work in public. The synthesis is the Einstein layer; the individual inputs are Grossmann.

**Verdict: adopt-now** — schedule deliberate cross-domain reading inputs (not AI papers) and make the synthesis visible on X. The "most human" posts are the ones that make unexpected connections no AI would generate.

### [CONFIRMS] §12 Positioning — Avoid the "Better-Trap" language

McCormick's "good slop" concept directly confirms the already-established prohibition on "-er-than-LangChain" language. "Faster, simpler, cleaner than LangChain" is good slop — it competes on the Grossmann layer. Verso's positioning must be at the Einstein layer: the irreducible judgment about what the harness should enforce and what it should not.

**Verdict: confirm** — no delta, already established.

### [NEW-IDEA] founder_os — "Room to breathe" as the operating principle for quality

McCormick's editorial restructuring (no fixed deadlines, sponsors without dates, ideas that develop over months) is an anti-deadline operating system. For a technical founder building in public, the failure mode is forcing a post for the sake of cadence (good slop) rather than waiting for the genuine insight. The True Fan model (Kelly) requires showing up regularly; the "most human wins" model requires showing up with Einstein-layer content. The tension between these two is the key operating tension.

**Resolution:** cadence for Grossmann-layer posts (short technical notes, code snippets, in-progress observations) but no deadline pressure for Einstein-layer posts (positioning essays, major technical arguments, Show HN launches). The former maintain presence; the latter build authority.

**Verdict: adopt-now** — distinguish post types by layer and apply different cadence/quality standards to each.
