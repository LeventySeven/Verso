## FAITHFUL DIGEST

Naval Ravikant's "How to Get Rich" is a tweetstorm (May 2018) that became one of the most widely shared pieces of founder/operator thinking of the era. It is not a get-rich-quick guide; it is a framework for understanding *the structural conditions* under which wealth becomes achievable in a technical knowledge economy. The key value for Verso is the **leverage taxonomy** — a rigorous classification of the four types of leverage and which ones are available to a technical founder building in public.

### Core Distinction: Wealth vs. Money vs. Status

Naval draws sharp definitional lines:
- **Wealth** = assets that earn while you sleep (equity, IP, software, media)
- **Money** = social credits representing past labor, transferable; a medium of exchange
- **Status** = position in social hierarchy; zero-sum (one person's gain is another's loss)

> "Seek wealth, not money or status."

Wealth creation is **positive-sum** — when Verso ships a better agent framework, the entire ecosystem benefits and wealth is created, unlike status competition which is inherently zero-sum. This has a specific implication: building in public and sharing knowledge is not giving away competitive advantage; it creates the positive-sum signal that attracts True Fans and establishes status within the right circles.

### The Wealth Formula

> "If you want to make money you have to get paid at scale. And why you — that's accountability, at scale — that's leverage, and just you getting paid as opposed to somebody else getting paid — that's specific knowledge."

Naval's formula: **Specific Knowledge + Accountability + Leverage + Judgment = Wealth**. Each component is load-bearing and maps directly to a technical founder's situation.

### 1. Specific Knowledge

> "Specific knowledge is knowledge that you cannot be trained for, and it cannot be outsourced."

> "Building specific knowledge will feel like play to you but will look like work to others."

Specific knowledge is not generic expertise. It emerges from genuine curiosity, unique life trajectory, and obsessive engagement — not formal education or credentials. It sits at the intersection of traits: your DNA, your upbringing, and your response to both. It is **the thing you do effortlessly that others find painful**.

Naval's key insight: specific knowledge is found by asking "what did I do as a kid almost effortlessly that now maps onto a valuable domain?" For a technical founder with deep agent framework intuition, this is structural awareness of how loops actually work in production — not textbook ML.

The implication for Verso positioning: the founder's specific knowledge (harness design, eval engineering, context density thinking) IS the product credibility. The X persona must express the specific knowledge authentically — the posts that feel like "just thinking out loud" to the author but look like expertise to readers.

### 2. Accountability

> "Accountability is reputational skin in the game."

> "If you take on accountability and solve problems on the edge of knowledge that others can't solve, people will line up behind you."

Accountability means putting your name on outcomes — not hiding behind corporate structures or pseudonymous accounts. Taking public positions on technical questions (harness vs. framework, context window engineering, eval-first design) and being right is how accountability compounds into leverage.

For a technical founder, accountability on X means: making specific technical claims about what works in production, being willing to be wrong publicly, and updating publicly when wrong. This is the behavior pattern that converts casual followers into True Fans (per Kelly).

### 3. Leverage — The Core Taxonomy

Naval identifies four types, split into two categories:

**Permissioned leverage** (requires others to grant access):
- **Labor** — people work for you. "Labor leverage is the worst form of leverage that you could possibly use." Hiring people requires managing people; it is expensive, slow, and creates its own bureaucracy.
- **Capital** — money works for you. Powerful but requires investors or prior wealth to acquire.

**Permissionless leverage** (no one needs to grant it; you can start today):
- **Code** — software works for you while you sleep. Zero marginal cost of replication. One engineer's code can serve 10 or 10 million users at nearly the same cost.
- **Media** — content (blog posts, essays, videos, podcasts, X threads) works for you while you sleep. Zero marginal cost of distribution. One essay can be read by 1,000 people or 1,000,000 people at the same cost to produce.

> "Code and media are permissionless leverage. They're the leverage behind the newly rich."

> "If you can't code, write books and blogs, record videos and podcasts."

The crucial insight: **code + media together are multiplicative, not additive**. A technical founder who builds an OSS framework (code leverage) AND builds in public on X (media leverage) has both forms of permissionless leverage compounding simultaneously. This is structurally different from a founder who only ships code or only writes.

### 4. Judgment

> "In an age of infinite leverage, judgment becomes the most important skill."

> "Wisdom is knowing the long-term consequences of your actions."

When leverage is democratized — when anyone can ship code and anyone can write — the differentiator is **which lever to pull, not the ability to pull levers**. Judgment is the highest-order skill because it determines where to apply the leverage.

For Verso, this maps to: the judgment to build an opinionated harness (not a generic framework), to make principled non-goals, to resist the temptation of graph abstractions, to pick the AI engineer persona over the ML researcher or vibe-coder. These are judgment calls that only someone with deep specific knowledge and accumulated experience can make correctly.

### Long-Term Games + Authenticity

> "Pick an industry where you can play long-term games with long-term people."

> "You can escape competition through authenticity when you realize that no one can compete with you on being you."

Naval's long-term games insight: trust and reputation compound in repeated interactions. Short-term thinking optimizes for individual interactions; long-term thinking optimizes for relationships. The X build-in-public play is fundamentally a long-term game — each post compounds trust, each technical position taken publicly compounds authority.

The authenticity escape: the reason counter-positioning works as a moat is not just that incumbents can't copy (Helmer's condition) — it is also that genuine expression is non-replicable. If the Verso brand voice is authentically the founder's specific knowledge expressed directly, it cannot be cloned by a team of content marketers.

### Society's Reward Mechanism

> "Society will pay you for creating things that it wants."

> "You will get rich by giving society what it wants but does not yet know how to get. At scale."

The "at scale" clause is key. Being brilliant in private creates no wealth. Being brilliant in a way that scales — through code, media, or both — is how wealth is generated. For Verso, "at scale" means: the harness design that works for the founder's specific production use case must also be packaged such that 10,000 structurally-aware AI engineers can run it without the founder present.

### The Permissionless Leverage Operational Detail

Naval's specific practical advice for the no-capital, no-team founder:

1. **Develop genuine specific knowledge** (not credential-chasing)
2. **Take on accountability publicly** (put your name on technical positions)
3. **Apply permissionless leverage** (code + media, starting today)
4. **Compound judgment** through reading, building, and shipping

The explicit note: "Apply specific knowledge, with leverage, and eventually you will get what you deserve." The word "eventually" is important — this is not a 6-month play but a 3-7 year compounding arc.

## VERSO RELEVANCE

### [SHARPENS] §12 GTM/Distribution — The Code+Media Multiplicative Stack

Naval's taxonomy sharpens the Verso GTM thesis in a specific way. The prior framework established "DX-led organic GTM" and "X build-in-public." Naval's taxonomy upgrades this: Verso has access to **both forms of permissionless leverage simultaneously** — OSS code (the framework, available on GitHub, earnable while the founder sleeps) AND media (X posts, essays, Show HN, README essays that compound authority). Most OSS projects use code leverage but underuse media leverage. Most content-heavy founders use media leverage but underuse code leverage. The combination is multiplicative.

**Operative implication:** every non-trivial harness design decision should produce two artifacts: (1) a code commit/PR, and (2) a corresponding X thread or short essay explaining the *judgment* behind the decision. The code is proof; the media is distribution.

**Verdict: adopt-now** — make the code+media pairing an operating norm from day one.

### [SHARPENS] §12 Positioning — Specific Knowledge as the non-replicable brand signal

Naval's specific knowledge concept sharpens why the Verso founder's personal X persona matters more than generic brand accounts. The founder's specific knowledge — the structural awareness of agent loops in production, the eval-first judgment, the harness-vs-framework distinction — is what makes the Verso positioning credible. Without the founder's name attached, the positioning becomes claims without proof. With it, it becomes: "the person who built X, shipped Y, wrote Z" is the frame through which Verso's technical positions are evaluated.

This is the reason "intellectual-honesty register" already established in prior passes is load-bearing: it is the expression of specific knowledge in the media channel.

**Verdict: sharpen** — the founder persona and the Verso brand must remain tightly coupled in the early phase. Do not genericize the voice too early.

### [CONFIRMS] founder_os — Permissionless leverage over permissioned leverage

Naval's framework confirms the founder-OS principle that early-stage technical founders should bias toward permissionless leverage (code + media) rather than permissioned leverage (raising capital, hiring). Verso's "personal-infra, dogfood-first" approach is permissionless leverage in practice — build for your own use case, make it code that runs without permission, document it in public. This is the correct early-stage leverage mix.

**Verdict: confirm** — no delta.

### [NEW-IDEA] founder_os — Judgment as the scarce resource to protect

Naval's "in an age of infinite leverage, judgment becomes the most important skill" has a specific implication for founder time allocation. If code leverage and media leverage are both available, the bottleneck is not shipping or writing — it is **exercising good judgment about what to ship and what to write**. This suggests: protect long thinking time, resist the pull toward reactive content or feature requests from the wrong users, and invest heavily in the signal quality of each post (not frequency). The judgment to say "we don't build X" (non-goals) is as valuable as the judgment to build Y.

**Verdict: adopt-now** — build "judgment preservation" into the operating system: scheduled deep work, no reactive feature prioritization, high bar for what gets a post vs. what stays internal.

### [SHARPENS] §1 Moat — Accountability as compounding reputation capital

Naval's accountability concept sharpens the "becoming-hunted" thesis. The mechanism is: take specific technical positions publicly → be right more often than wrong → accumulate reputational skin-in-game → create a reputation that "attracts opportunities deterministically." For Verso, this means making specific, falsifiable claims about harness design in public (not just "our harness is better" but "here is exactly why context density matters, here is the measurement, here is what you'd lose without it"). Falsifiable specificity is accountability; vague benefit claims are not.

**Verdict: adopt-now** — every major Verso technical post should contain at least one falsifiable, specific claim. The accountability is the trust-building mechanism.

### [SHARPENS] §12 GTM — "Escape competition through authenticity"

Naval's authenticity-escape maps to the Verso counter-positioning logic. The anti-framework counter-position (Anthropic "use the APIs directly" + Chase "traded power for ease" + Ronacher "build your own abstraction") is not primarily a competitive analysis — it is an expression of genuine specific knowledge. The founder genuinely believes these things because they've built production agents and seen the failure modes. The authenticity is the moat; a content team could never produce the same signal.

**Verdict: confirm/sharpen** — the authenticity escape is already implicit in the intellectual-honesty register but should be made explicit in how posts are written: show the reasoning, not just the conclusion. "Escape competition through authenticity" is the warrant for not writing polished marketing copy.
