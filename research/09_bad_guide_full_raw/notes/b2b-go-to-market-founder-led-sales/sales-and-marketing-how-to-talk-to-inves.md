## Part 2: Michael Seibel (YC) — Investor Pitch Frameworks

### The Core Claim
Only two pitch lengths work: 30 seconds and 2 minutes.
> "30-minute pitches, hour pitches are all garbage."

Founders over-prepare for long pitches and under-prepare for conversational shorts. The 30-second pitch is the most important thing to master — it governs every hallway conversation, every cold intro, every first email.

---

### The 30-Second Pitch (3 sentences)

**Sentence 1 — What your company does:**
One sentence. Must pass the "Mom test" — comprehensible without domain knowledge or AI context. No jargon.

**Sentence 2 — Market size:**
Multi-billion dollar reference is the floor. Not a bottom-up number — just establish that the ceiling is large. "Share something that the biggest players in your market don't understand or do well."

**Sentence 3 — Traction:**
- Post-launch: specific growth metrics (MoM %, revenue, users)
- Pre-launch: velocity signals ("started in January, launched beta in March, product in April")
> "Share something that demonstrates extreme speed."

---

### The 2-Minute Pitch (adds 4 elements)

**4. Unique insight (2 sentences):**
Share something investors don't know. This is the moment Seibel is watching for: "You can see it on the investor's face." If the insight doesn't visibly land, it wasn't a real insight.

**5. Business model (1 sentence):**
Pick one model and state it. Do not hedge. Hedging = you don't know. "Clarify revenue mechanism (advertising, direct sales, etc.)"

**6. Team (2 sentences):**
- Have you made investors money before? If yes, say so.
- If no: How many founders? How many technical? How long known each other? All full-time?

**7. The ask:**
Know the instrument (SAFE vs. convertible note, cap, amount, minimum check). Use the jargon. Not knowing the instrument signals you're not ready. Example from roleplay: "$500K convertible at $8.5M cap."

---

### Power Dynamics: Flipping the Investor/Founder Equation

Seibel's meta-point: most founders approach fundraising from a position of perceived weakness. The remedies:
- Demonstrate team commitment (all full-time, history of working together)
- Show pre-launch progress (velocity, not just vision)
- Signal that you're building-focused, not money-desperate
> "Signal via email that you're building-focused, not money-desperate."

## Part 3: Dalton Caldwell + Qasar Younis — Investor Meeting Roleplay

### The Mock Pitch (Communication Platform Example)

The roleplay features a "bad" pitch followed by critique and an improved pitch.

**Mistakes identified in first iteration:**
- Vague product explanation (couldn't understand what it does)
- Evasive on specific numbers when asked
- Weak insight articulation (stated an observation, not a real asymmetric insight)
- Poor conversation direction (let the investor take control instead of steering)

**Improvements in second iteration:**
- Clearer use cases with specific names (Google/Facebook/Yelp integration for distribution)
- Specific growth metrics stated upfront
- Focused market analysis (removed hand-waving about "all communications")
- Urgency signaling ("closing Friday") — legitimate when real competitive pressure exists

---

### Fundraising Mechanics (Dalton/Qasar)

**Raise breadth-first, not sequentially:**
Schedule all investor meetings in the same 1–2 week window. Do not run sequential processes. Hack: "We're building like crazy for the next 2 weeks — can we schedule for week 3?" This:
- Groups all meetings simultaneously
- Signals you're not desperate
- Creates social proof and competitive heat

**One team member goes full-time on fundraising:**
Raises are too distracting split across the team. Designate one person for the compressed window.

**Interpreting investor signals:**
- Anything other than a wire/check is a no
- "Keep talking to partners" = no
- "Let's schedule a follow-up" after a cold first meeting without specific ask = no

**The final caution:**
> "Fundraising is essential, but it is not the goal. Just because you've got a fund raise does not mean you succeeded. Build the product!"

(Dalton): "A lot of smart people equate fundraising success with company success. Fundraising is not the goal. Build your company."

### §12 Positioning: The Mom test as a legibility ceiling check [SHARPENS]
Seibel's "one sentence, comprehensible without domain knowledge" test is a direct sharpener of VERSO's legibility ceiling problem. VERSO's harness positioning uses jargon ("seam," "context engineering," "tactical↔durable") that fails the Mom test. The correct move is not to abandon the vocabulary — it is to have a Mom-test sentence that precedes the jargon for investor conversations: "VERSO is the scaffolding that makes AI agents reliable enough to run in production, without forcing you to rewrite them as your requirements change."
**Verdict:** Sharpens legibility-ceiling framing. VERSO needs a Mom-test sentence distinct from its technical positioning vocabulary.

Seibel's test — "you can see it on the investor's face when the insight lands" — is a generalizable taste test for any VERSO claim. Apply it: state each positioning claim in the 2-minute pitch and watch for the face. If the room is polite but unresponsive, the claim is not an insight — it's a statement of opinion.
