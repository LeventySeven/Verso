## Core Argument

Cranney's foundational reframe: **sales creates new value for customers, it does not merely communicate existing value.** The conventional view is that sales communicates your product's existing features and advantages. Cranney argues the high-end field sales motion works because it surfaces customer problems customers haven't fully articulated — problems you can solve if you understand their organizational cascade from corporate objectives down to individual capabilities.

Opening quote (verbatim from Cranney):
> "The art of persuasion is a paradox — the more we attempt to persuade people, the more they tend to resist us. But the more we attempt to understand and create value for them, the more they tend to persuade themselves."

This is not a technique tip. It is a structural framing: the best enterprise sales processes are discovery-and-value-creation processes, not persuasion processes.

## The 7-Step Field Sales Framework

### Step 1: Identify Initiatives
Map the customer's organizational priorities: Corporate objectives → Business strategies → Initiatives → Critical capabilities → Solution set. Never start at the solution level. Start at the corporate objectives level and cascade down.

Buyer tiers with distinct priorities:
- **Executives:** shareholder value, revenue, net income (corporate objectives)
- **Directors/VPs:** initiatives and critical capabilities (integration, security, standards)
- **ICs:** features and functionality

### Step 2: Create Value Proposition
Value proposition must be mapped against the organizational cascade — not generic. The same product must be presented at three different levels for three different audiences, each speaking to their layer's concerns.

Key concept: **comparative vs. differentiated positioning**
- Comparative: what you share with competition
- Differentiated: what competitors lack

The competitive advantage lives in what only you can do at the capability layer that maps to their specific initiative.

### Step 3: Execute Competitive Strategy (Sun Tzu-inspired)
Four strategies per competitor, chosen based on your position:

- **Direct:** Overwhelming superiority — compete head-to-head
- **Divide (Indirect):** Win a portion of the business while coexisting; common in political situations inside accounts
- **Develop:** Intentionally slow momentum if unready; position for future strength
- **Defend:** Protect existing gains against a specific threat

Critical: "The great general establishes a position where he cannot be defeated before conflict begins." Pre-positioning matters more than in-fight tactics.

### Step 4: Validate Solution (Technical Validation Events)
Technical validation events are **not demos** — they are business-case generators.

Validation event types:
1. Freemium/premium: quick value intro, minimal resources
2. RFI/RFP: narrowing the competitive field
3. Demo: comprehensive, uses customer data, supports value prop
4. Proof of Concept (POC): 2-3 day test drive or 2-4 week extensive evaluation
5. Pilot: aims for production transition

**CRUSH All POCs (CAP) Methodology:**
1. Qualification — confirm technical success creates a sale; define success criteria
2. Preparation — assemble teams, dry runs
3. Execution — at customer site, leverage SMEs
4. Follow Through — written summary, address open items
5. Continuous Improvement — internal post-mortem

ROI quantification happens **during** validation, not after:
> "There's no better time to gather financial metrics...than during the technical validation event process."

### Step 5: Propose Solution
Multi-option proposals, not single quotes. Show:
- Initial deployment scope
- Phased expansion possibilities
- Full environment total cost of ownership (TCO)

This prevents friction later and signals long-term partnership vision.

### Step 6: Negotiate and Close
Quantified ROI provides the negotiating anchor:
> "It's a little tougher to beat me over the head on price...if I've got use cases that are going to have a higher financial impact than a competitor."

Deal review involves: sales management, customer success, sales engineering, marketing, finance, legal, ROI/business case team.

### Step 7: Deploy + Develop
The easiest place to sell is where you've already sold. Deployment success creates expansion and renewal opportunities. Customer success integration with the sales process begins before close.

## Champion / Coach / Supporter Distinctions

These are not interchangeable — they are categorically different in advocacy level:

**Champion:** "Somebody who has the influence, capability, and desire to sell on my behalf" during critical internal votes. Champions actively fight for your solution when you're not in the room.

**Coach:** Provides tactical guidance on internal processes but won't advocate openly. Valuable for intelligence-gathering, not for closing.

**Supporter:** Will cast a vote for you but offers no advance competitive advantage.

Blond's rule: continuously assess and re-assess. Champions can degrade to coaches mid-deal. Identifying internal "enemies" (those actively blocking you) is as important as cultivating allies.

Account penetration landscape uses Miller Heiman framework:
- Economic buyer (one per sale)
- User buyer (several)
- Technical buyer (several)
- Coach (shares information, doesn't openly fight for you)

## Why-Do-Anything / Why-You / Why-Now Framework

The three sequential buyer questions that must be answered in order:

1. **"Why should I do anything different?"** — Establishing need/change motivation
2. **"Why should I choose you over competition?"** — Differentiation (your unique capability)
3. **"Why now?"** — Urgency and ROI justification

Cranney emphasizes this sequencing is non-negotiable. A buyer who can't answer Q1 will never authentically engage Q2 or Q3. Most enterprise sales reps skip to Q2 without establishing Q1.

> "The technical validation event is to provide clarity on answers for why you and why now."

## RFP / RFI Pre-Seeding Strategy

If you receive an RFP you haven't influenced, you've probably wasted your time:
> "If I'm getting an RFI or an RFP and I haven't influenced it, I've probably wasted my time."

Pre-seeding tactics:
- Build "customer requirements documents" (requirements PRDs) to seed into accounts early
- Create evaluation documents and POC plans that become the evaluation criteria
- Use technical validation events to set traps for competitors — criteria they cannot meet
- Understand the RFP/RFI "in very, very, very granular detail" to influence language before it publishes

---

## ROI Framework

Two impact buckets:
- **Hard dollars:** direct cost reduction, risk mitigation
- **Soft dollars:** productivity gains, revenue augmentation

Present three scenarios: base case, conservative, aggressive. Different stakeholders respond to different cases — executives like possibility; evaluators need numbers they can defend.
