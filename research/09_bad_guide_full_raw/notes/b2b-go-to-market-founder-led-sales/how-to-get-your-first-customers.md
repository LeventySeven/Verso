## Core Argument

The foundational thesis: **"If you build it, they won't come."** Startups succeed because founders manually recruit their customers. The biggest mistake is assuming software distributes itself, or that conversion optimization fixes what is actually an outreach volume problem.

The talk is structured in three sections:
1. Do Things That Don't Scale (the mindset frame)
2. Founder-led sales mechanics (the funnel, the email, the close)
3. Working backwards from your goals (the volume math)

Alstromer references Paul Graham's "Do Things That Don't Scale" essay as the most important essay about very early-stage startups. The key YC insight from the Airbnb story: when PG asked the founders where their customers were and they said "New York," he told them to go there immediately rather than pitch him. Physical presence and manual effort are not embarrassing — they are the advantage.

## Why Founders Must Do Sales First

- Learning sales gives founders "full control of your destiny as a startup"
- Founders should not outsource sales until they understand what repeatable success looks like
- A love for solving customer problems is infectious — customers respond to founder authenticity differently than they respond to reps
- Founders without sales experience can learn it; it is a learnable skill, especially when you genuinely understand the problem

Key YC companies that succeeded with early outbound: Amplitude, Stripe, Front — all ran outbound email campaigns at the very beginning.

## The Sales Funnel (Simplified)

1. Make a list of target customers
2. Send outreach emails/messages
3. Schedule demos/meetings
4. Discuss pricing
5. **Onboard** (critical step most founders skip — you must track this step)

The funnel is not useful as a list. It is useful as a **diagnostic**: identify at which step you lose the most, then fix that step. Most founders never instrument step 5.

---

## Customer Acquisition Channels

Primary sourcing in order of ease:
1. **Warm introductions** — co-workers, friends, former colleagues, investors
2. **LinkedIn outreach**
3. **Reddit, Slack, Discord communities, Facebook groups** — niche communities with self-selecting early adopters
4. **In-person events** — for high-touch B2B contexts

**Strategic targeting principle:** Pursue the easiest customers first. Do not start with Fortune 500 prospects. Start with startups (shorter decision cycles, fewer procurement hurdles) and people you know.

Note on channels: Alstromer does NOT frame content marketing or community-building as early-stage first-customer acquisition channels. These are listed as places to find early users, not as scalable acquisition strategies. Cold outbound and warm intro are the operative mechanisms at Stage-1.

## The Sales Email

Effective outreach:
- 6-8 sentences maximum
- Plain text only (no HTML, no branded formatting)
- Address the prospect's specific problem (not your product's features)
- Establish credibility: identify yourself as founder + social proof signal
- Include direct link to demo or product
- Clear CTA (one action, not multiple)

The goal of the email is to get a demo call, not to explain the product.

## Working Backwards from Goals

The biggest mistake most founders make is insufficient outreach volume, because they don't do the math. Alstromer demonstrates:

If you need 5 closed customers:
- 20% close rate from demo → need 25 demos
- 50% demo-to-conversation rate → need 50 conversations
- 5% email response rate → need **1,000 outreach emails**

Most founders send 100-200 emails, get ~5 replies, conclude the approach doesn't work, and pivot to a product explanation. The real cause is insufficient volume. The funnel math proves the required inputs before you can conclude strategy failure.

## Charging From Day One

> "If you don't charge your customers, they are not a customer."

Free users do not validate product-market fit. Payment signals genuine need. If a prospect won't pay, move to the next prospect. For B2B:
- Offer money-back guarantees or annual contracts with opt-out provisions if prospects are hesitant
- Annual contracts with early opt-out are better than free trials for commitment signaling

## PMF Signal: Retention, Not Conversion

(Recovered from broader Alstromer body of work / related YC context — 2018 growth talk and consistent theme):

PMF shows up in retention, not in conversion rate. A product with strong initial conversion but high early churn does not have PMF — it has good marketing. PMF is identified when cohorts of users/customers continue using the product without active persuasion. This is why step 5 (onboarding) in the funnel is critical: you cannot measure retention without tracking what happens post-close.

---

## Customer Qualification During Calls

Ask during discovery:
- What problem are they solving?
- Why solve it now?
- Do they currently pay for solutions to this problem?
- Are they the decision-maker?

Qualifying early eliminates low-intent prospects and concentrates effort on high-probability closes.

---

## Sales Conversation Best Practices

- Listen more than you speak
- Schedule next steps **during** the meeting, not afterward via email (prevents disengagement and commitment avoidance)
- Do not send demos by email — walk them through the product live
