## What Hormozi Actually Argues

### Core Claim: The Offer Is the Unit of Competition
Hormozi's thesis is that most founders compete on marketing, persuasion, or product features—but the actual leverage point is the *offer*. The offer determines whether prospects convert and at what margin. The same $10,000 in advertising yields:
- ~$5,000 revenue with a commoditized offer
- ~$112,000 revenue with a Grand Slam Offer

The implication: offer construction precedes and outweighs all other GTM activities.

### The Grand Slam Offer
Defined as "a combination of pricing, value, guarantees, and naming strategies designed to make a product or service so compelling that prospects feel foolish declining it."

A Grand Slam Offer is not a product—it is the packaging of a product into a solution that addresses the dream outcome and removes the specific obstacles preventing customers from achieving it.

### The Success Hierarchy
> "Market > Offer > Persuasion"

Market selection is the first lever. A great market generates revenue despite a mediocre offer and mediocre salesmanship. A terrible market destroys all leverage.

**Four market selection criteria:**
1. **Massive Pain** — the problem must be acutely felt, not merely inconvenient
2. **Purchasing Power** — the target audience must be able to afford the solution
3. **Easy to Target** — prospects must be findable through concentrated channels
4. **Growth** — the market must be expanding

**The three eternal markets:** Health, Wealth, Relationships — these always have buyers.

### The Value Equation

**Formula:**  
`Value = (Dream Outcome × Perceived Likelihood of Achievement) / (Perceived Time Delay × Perceived Effort & Sacrifice)`

The four variables:

| Variable | Direction | What it means in practice |
|---|---|---|
| Dream Outcome | Maximize | The specific future state the buyer actually wants, not what you're selling |
| Perceived Likelihood | Maximize | Confidence buyer will achieve the outcome (testimonials, guarantees, social proof matter more than actual success rate) |
| Time Delay | Minimize | Time from purchase to first meaningful result; perceived time is as important as actual time |
| Effort & Sacrifice | Minimize | Perceived cost of effort required from the buyer |

**The key insight:** The denominator drives infinite leverage. If Perceived Time Delay → 0 and Perceived Effort → 0, value → ∞. This is why "done for you" outperforms "taught you how" in almost every market.

**Perception caveat:** "Perceived likelihood of success matters more than actual success rates when it comes to purchase decisions." A 50% actual success rate with strong testimonials and guarantees outperforms a 95% success rate with no social proof.

### Five-Step Grand Slam Offer Construction

1. **Identify the Dream Outcome** — what specific result does the buyer actually want? (Not "a gym membership" but "to lose 20 lbs before summer")
2. **List All Obstacles** — every barrier the buyer faces before, during, and after using the product
3. **Reverse Problems** — transform each obstacle into a solution-delivery component
4. **Brainstorm Delivery Vehicles** — for each solution, choose the interaction model using the Delivery Cube: one-on-one / group / many × synchronous / async × live / recorded × digital / physical
5. **Stack and Bundle** — remove low-value high-cost components; bundle remaining into a cohesive package

The result: a multi-component offer where each element removes a specific obstacle, rather than a single product feature list.

### Premium Pricing Logic
> "Charge as high as you can say without cracking a smile."

> "Those who pay the most, pay the most attention."

> "The pain is the pitch."

Higher prices trigger a "virtuous cycle": client pays more → invests more effort → gets better results → becomes better testimonial → validates higher prices for future clients. Lowering prices triggers the reverse cycle.

Hormozi argues founders should price *higher* than feels comfortable specifically to test whether customers genuinely need the solution. If a customer pushes back on price but won't walk away, they need it.

### Five Enhancement Tools

**Scarcity** — Constrain supply through capacity caps, cohort sizes, or growth-rate limits. Not fake scarcity—real constraints that signal quality control.

**Urgency** — Time-bound the offer via seasonal windows, bonus expiration dates, or cohort deadlines. ~50-60% of weekly sales occur in the final four hours of a deadline window.

**Bonuses** — Stack additions that are positioned as separate high-value components, not features of the base offer. "The more you add, the more people value the offer" — but only if each addition is valued independently.

**Guarantees** — Four types:
- *Unconditional* (money-back, no questions): eliminates purchase risk entirely
- *Conditional* (refund if buyer does X steps): filters committed buyers and reduces refund rate
- *Anti-guarantee* (all sales final): signals confidence and filters for serious buyers
- *Implied* (revenue share / outcome-based): aligns incentives and signals belief in the outcome

**Naming (M-A-G-I-C formula):**
- **M** = Magnetic reason why (the mechanism that makes the outcome achievable)
- **A** = Avatar (who specifically this is for)
- **G** = Goal (the dream outcome)
- **I** = Interval (time to result)
- **C** = Container word (challenge, blueprint, system, program, accelerator, bootcamp)
