## Faithful Digest

### Sales Defined Simply

Steli's grounding frame: "Sales is nothing more than results-driven communication." He argues that founders already possess sales skills through everyday interactions — the barrier to founder-led sales is psychological, not capability-based. This is important because it removes the objection that "technical founders can't sell."

### Cold Email: Three Questions, One CTA

The structure of an effective cold email must answer three questions:
1. Who are you?
2. What do you want?
3. Why contact this specific person?

Followed by a single, specific call to action. Steli explicitly warns against manipulative subject lines that inflate open rates — a 30-40% open rate is healthy for legitimate outbound; 15% is the minimum before reconsidering the approach. Response rate benchmark among openers: 10-20%.

The key insight: **specificity signals respect**. A vague email says "I didn't think about you specifically." A specific email says "I researched you and believe this is relevant." The former gets deleted; the latter gets replies.

### Cold Calling: Tone Over Content

For cold calls, tone matters more than content. Being nervous or aggressive undermines even strong messaging. The recommendation: be excited, clear, concise, composed. Answer rates approximately 15%; conversion among those who answer approximately 15%. The implication is that cold calling at scale is a numbers game, but cold calling to specific high-value targets is a tone game.

### Pricing: Charge More

Steli's pricing heuristic: find the price where approximately 20% of prospects think it's too expensive, 20% think it's cheap, and 60% genuinely weigh value vs. cost. If no one ever complains about price, you're undercharging and signaling low confidence.

"Charge more" as a first principle: most early-stage founders underprice because they're insecure about value. The discipline of charging real prices filters serious buyers and creates a feedback loop where only users who believe in the value continue — which improves retention and reduces churn from low-commitment users.

### Urgency: Real, Not Manufactured

Rather than artificial promotional deadlines, Steli recommends connecting your product to the customer's genuine business priorities. Understand what matters urgently to them; demonstrate that your product moves that metric. Fake urgency ("this offer expires Friday") is recognized and creates distrust. Real urgency ("you said Q3 pipeline is your bottleneck — here's how we fix that this month") creates genuine pressure.

### The Medical Diagnosis Model of Selling

The sales process Steli recommends mirrors medical diagnosis:
1. **Diagnose deeply** — understand the customer's actual situation before presenting solutions
2. **Build credibility** through the quality of your questions, not your pitch
3. **Prescribe confidently** — the doctor doesn't apologize for the recommendation; they explain why it's right
4. **Follow through and champion** — closing isn't the end; you're an internal champion for the customer's success

Most salespeople skip diagnosis and jump to selling. This destroys trust because the customer can tell they're not being heard.

### Enterprise Pilots: Always Charge

Even at early stage, charge for pilots. The amount can be nominal; it can be fully refundable; it can be held separately. The psychological effect of payment commitment matters more than the revenue. A free pilot gets no internal priority — there's no skin in the game. A paid pilot, even at $1, creates accountability on the customer side.

### Scaling a Sales Team: Four Stages

1. **Master repeatable, teachable methods** before hiring — you can't train what you can't articulate
2. **Hire junior salespeople in pairs or small teams** — identify which approaches work before optimizing
3. **Add a junior sales manager** (someone 1-2 years ahead of where you are) who has managed at your current stage, not at 10x your size
4. **Hire experienced VP/senior leadership** when the team reaches 15-20 people

The warning embedded here: founders hire senior sales leadership too early, before they understand their own sales motion. A VP of Sales with enterprise experience will build an enterprise sales process — which is wrong for a PLG or developer-tool company at seed stage.

### International Expansion: English First

Start with English-speaking international customers to avoid localization complexity. Product and pricing can remain in your native language and currency. Simple optimizations like localized landing pages can test demand before deeper investment.

## VERSO RELEVANCE

Steli's AMA is the most sales-tactical of the batch. Verso's GTM is DX-led organic, not a traditional sales motion — but several findings apply to the founder-led pre-PMF phase and to how Verso should think about its first paid tier.

### [CONFIRMS] §12 GTM — Founder-Led Sales Is the Right Pre-PMF Mode

Steli's argument that "founders already have sales skills" through everyday communication confirms the founder-led sales approach at early stage. For Verso, this means the first revenue conversations happen with the founder present — not handed to a salesperson. This is consistent with the established DX-led organic GTM.

*Tag: [CONFIRMS] §12 GTM*  
*Verdict: confirm*

### [NEW-IDEA] §12 GTM — The Medical Diagnosis Model Maps to the "Live Wedge Session"

The diagnosis-first sales model has a direct Verso analog. In the live wedge session (from the "Do Things That Don't Scale" note), the founder is present while a senior engineer integrates Verso. The diagnosis frame says: **don't start by showing the feature; start by asking "what does your agent loop look like right now? what breaks?"** The quality of that question builds credibility before a single line of Verso code runs.

This reframes the live wedge session from a demo to a diagnostic — which is a more durable positioning move. The engineer trusts you because you understand their problem, not because your demo is slick.

*Tag: [NEW-IDEA] §12 GTM / §7 demo-able wedge*  
*Verdict: adopt-now — redesign the live wedge session opening as diagnostic, not demo; first 5 minutes = "tell me about your loop" not "here's what Verso does"*

### [SHARPENS] §12 Pricing — "Charge More" Applies to the First Paid Tier Design

Steli's pricing heuristic — 20% think it's too expensive, 20% think it's cheap, 60% weigh it genuinely — is directly applicable to Verso's first paid tier design. If Verso's early pricing creates no friction at all (everyone says yes immediately), it's underpriced and attracting low-commitment users who will churn when the first rough edge appears. The 20% who find it "too expensive" are actually providing a signal that the price is credible.

The implication for Verso: the first paid tier should be priced to create mild friction, not to maximize conversion. Maximize for user quality, not user count, in the first cohort.

*Tag: [SHARPENS] §12 GTM / positioning*  
*Verdict: sharpen — design first paid tier price to create mild friction; treat "too expensive" complaints as confirmation, not problems*

### [NEW-IDEA] founder_os — Never Hire Senior Sales Before You Can Articulate the Motion

Steli's four-stage scaling framework contains a specific warning: founders hire senior sales leadership before they understand their own sales motion. For Verso, this maps to a broader principle: **don't hire for a function you haven't yet mastered personally**. The DX-led organic loop must produce repeatable user acquisition before any hire that tries to systematize it. Otherwise you'll hire someone who builds the wrong system.

*Tag: [NEW-IDEA] founder_os*  
*Verdict: adopt-now — delay any sales/growth hire until founder can articulate: "here is our repeatable acquisition sequence, here is what triggers conversion, here is what causes churn"*

### [SHARPENS] §12 GTM — Specificity in Cold Outreach = "I Thought About You Specifically"

Steli's "specificity signals respect" principle sharpens Verso's outreach approach for superconsumers. The DX-led organic GTM targets senior/AI engineers — but cold outreach to that persona (GitHub issues, DMs, Show HN comments) should be specific about *why this person* and *what specific problem of theirs* Verso addresses. Generic "check out my framework" messages signal low effort. Specific "I saw your thread about agent loop reliability — this is exactly the seam problem Verso solves" messages signal that you understand their situation.

*Tag: [SHARPENS] §12 GTM / distribution*  
*Verdict: adopt-now — craft person-specific cold outreach templates for superconsumer targets; cite their specific public content*
