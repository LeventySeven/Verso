## Core Argument

Sam Blond's thesis is that the most common startup sales failures are not mysterious — they stem from executing obvious things backwards. Most founders misdiagnose their growth constraint: they assume conversion is the problem when the real issue is insufficient demand. Everything downstream flows from this error.

> "Focus on demand until you have too much."

The talk is structured as nine practitioner concepts, each of which most founders execute in reverse.

---

## The Nine Concepts (with substance)

### 1. Don't Outsource Recruiting
External recruiters produce weaker hires. Top candidates arrive through referrals — they carry accountability to the person who recommended them. Internal recruiting is everyone's job, not a silo. At Brex, there are no referral bonuses because hiring is treated as core professional responsibility, not optional.

Key sequencing: never hire two first reps simultaneously from external sources. Hire two internally-referred reps together so you can diagnose whether failure is individual or systemic.

### 2. Focus on Demand Until You Have Too Much
The primary metric is not conversion rate on existing pipeline — it is: **"How many new opportunities were you able to create this week?"**

Most startups misidentify their constraint as conversion/closing when it is actually demand generation and opportunity creation. You cannot solve stalled sales by hiring more AEs — you spread limited demand across more mouths, destroying per-rep productivity. Hire more reps only when the growth constraint is "not enough salespeople to handle existing demand."

Do not hire SDRs before proving outbound works with AEs directly. AEs must validate the outbound motion first.

### 3. Do Things That Stand Out
Creative demand generation beats generic outbound. Blond's two recurring examples:
- **Brex's champagne campaign**: high impact at low cost — personalized, surprising, contrarian
- **Salesforce's competitor conference protest**: theatrical but memorable

Quarterly self-check: "What 2-3 creative campaigns did we execute this quarter?"

### 4. Leverage Happy Customers to Generate New Ones
Most founders send a thank-you email and move on. Delight customers with personalized gestures — this is one of the most underutilized acquisition channels. Happy customers generate organic referrals and social advocacy. The channel compounds without cold-outreach costs.

### 5. Distribution Equals Announcement
A great product launch fails without distribution work equal to the product work. Tactics:
- Map influential networks before launch day
- Coordinate social amplification simultaneously on launch
- Treat distribution as a parallel workstream, not an afterthought

"Great announcements need thoughtful distribution strategy to create real impact."

### 6. Be Prescriptive on Purchase Process
Educate prospects on how to buy your product. If you don't know the path, they certainly won't. Remove ambiguity from pricing, demos, and onboarding from day one. Most founders assume buyers intuit the path — they don't.

### 7. The Presumptive Close
For transactional sales (<30-day cycles) and free trials: guide prospects through the actual sign-up / setup process **during the demo**. Act as though they are already a customer. At Brex, this approach increased conversions **2-3x**. The mechanic: close at the end of the demo call by walking them through the application, not by scheduling a follow-up.

### 8. Meet Prospects In Person
In-person meetings increase conversion likelihood by **~3x** versus virtual-only selling. Most competitors default to video calls — in-person meetings differentiate by signaling commitment and willingness to go above expectations. This is a competitive differentiator most founders overlook post-COVID.

### 9. Obsess Over Implementation
You only get one chance to make a first impression with a new customer. Specialized onboarding teams with first-30-day milestones predict lifetime value, churn probability, and NPS better than post-sale CSM handoffs. Don't deprioritize customer experience during hypergrowth (Blond cites Zenefits' mistake of acquisition focus at implementation's expense).

## Sequencing and Structure Concepts (from broader Q&A)

- **Hiring readiness test:** When you have "a handful of non-friends-and-family revenue customers with a repeatable process," you are ready for your first salesperson. Not before.
- **PLG is not a patch:** You cannot solve stalled sales through product-led growth if you've lost product-market fit.
- **Revenue sourcing hierarchy:** Highest ROI activity = closing revenue. Second highest = sourcing revenue. SDR work amplifies only after the AE-led motion is proven.
- **Concentric circle outreach:** Your network → investor networks → employee networks → cold outbound. In that order.
- **14-day rule:** In founder-led sales, you'll know within two weeks if a new hire works out.
- **70%+ quota target:** If less than 70% of your team hits quota, the OTE itself is meaningless.
