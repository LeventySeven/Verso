## Core Argument

Founders have a natural sales advantage — they combine passion with deep product expertise — so one founder should personally own all early sales before any sales hire. The job before product-market fit is not to close everyone; it is to find the 2.5% who will buy now, learn fast, and not waste cycles on "maybes."

## Framework 1: The Sales Funnel (4 Stages)

**Prospecting → Conversations → Closing → Revenue**

### Prospecting
- Only ~2.5% of companies are "innovators" willing to bet on an unproven product. This means 97.5% will say no no matter what you do — accept it as math, not a verdict on your product.
- Sources: personal network + referrals from friends-of-friends; targeted industry conferences (obtain attendee list in advance, pre-book 30-min meetings); cold email.
- **Cold email template (verbatim distillation):**
  > "Hey, I'm [Name]. This is what we do. I thought this might be relevant for XYZ reason. Do you have time this week?"
  - Must be: short, personalized, genuine, non-salesy, and end with a clear call-to-action. No buzzwords, no feature lists.

### Conversations
- "Sales is about listening. Your instinct as a founder is to talk most of the time, but you have to fight that."
- Top sales people listen 70%, speak 30%. Bosmeny recommends UberConference to track talk-time ratios.
- Core discovery questions: "What's your problem? How do you solve it today? Why did you take my call? What's your ideal solution?"
- Goal: understand the pain, not pitch the product.

### Closing
- Get to yes or no as fast as possible. **"No" is better than "Maybe."** Maybes block your pipeline; explicit rejections let you reallocate.
- **The feature request trap:** When a prospect says "I'll buy if you add X," this is usually a disguised "no." Only build it if multiple customers ask, or if it's your first/second customer. Never build for a single gatekeeper without a signed conditional commitment.
- **Free trial alternative:** Never offer free trials — they produce no revenue, no commitment, and no validation. Instead offer: "30-day cancellation clause on an annual contract." This secures commitment, reduces buyer anxiety, and gives you real signal.
- Have legal templates pre-drafted (YC provides open-source sales agreements) so you can move instantly when a prospect says yes.

### Revenue
- Paid commitment is the only validation signal that matters.
- "A free trial doesn't get you any of those things" — validation, commitment, revenue.

## Framework 2: The $100M Revenue Matrix (Christoph Janz)

A map of how to reach $100M ARR based on ARPU vs. customer count:

| ARPU | Customers needed | Sales motion |
|---|---|---|
| $100K | 1,000 | Direct enterprise sales |
| $10K | 10,000 | Inside sales |
| $1K | 100,000 | Self-serve / SMB |
| $100 | 1,000,000 | Viral / product-led |
| $10 | 10,000,000 | Consumer / not viable via direct sales |

Implication for founders: your price point determines your sales motion. If you price too low, you cannot afford a sales team; if you price for enterprise, you need relationships and full-cycle selling.

## Framework 3: Follow-Up Cadence

- Non-responses are not rejections. Prospects are busy; they ignore emails while still interested.
- Follow up every 5–7 days without apology. "It's not over till they say no."
- One actual Clever deal required multiple touchpoints over two months before closing a $100K annual contract.
- Only an explicit "no" lets you close the lead and move on.
- **"You're going to hear no a lot!"** — reframe this as the game, not evidence of product failure.

---

## Framework 4: Hiring Sales People

- Do NOT hire a dedicated salesperson until you have personally executed sales and understand exactly what works. You will hire wrong if you skip this.
- Seek "Renaissance reps" (high energy, love problem-solving, moderate industry experience, hustle-first) over "coin-operated" reps (playbook-dependent, require commission clarity and scripts).
- You'll only understand the required skill profile through direct selling.
- Quote: "You should be spending every moment of every day doing one of two things: building a product or talking to users." — Paul Graham
