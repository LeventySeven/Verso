## Core Argument

Janz expands on Boris Wertz's binary (1,000 enterprise customers vs. 1M consumers) into a logarithmic five-tier framework: the animal you hunt determines your entire GTM machine before you write the first pitch deck or make the first hire. The central math: to reach $100M ARR you need either 1,000 customers at $100k+, 10,000 at $10k+, 100,000 at $1k+, 1M at $100+, or 10M ad-supported users at $10+ effective ARPA. Each tier is named after an animal whose size corresponds to deal size.

The 2019 update (based on 74 public SaaS unicorns) found two-thirds of unicorns hunt "deer" ($3,300–$33,000 ARPA) or "elephants" ($33,000–$333,000 ARPA) — the middle tiers dominate real outcomes.

## The Five Animals — Full Detail

### Flies ($10 effective ARPA, 10M users, ad-supported)
- Requires ~100M downloads or website visitors
- Only two mechanisms scale: (a) inherent social virality/viral coefficient (Instagram, Snapchat, WhatsApp), (b) massive user-generated content with SEO (Yelp, Brainly)
- No sales motion at all — pure product/distribution play

### Mice (<$333 ARPA / ~$100/yr consumers)
- 1M paying customers required; 10–20M trial users to convert
- CAC < $40 (50% churn, $170 LTV)
- Viral mechanics still essential; MailChimp, Evernote as examples
- 10M trial signups needed at 10% conversion; no sales budget permissible — fully self-service

### Rabbits ($333–$3,300 ARPA / ~$1k/yr small biz)
- 100,000 small business customers required
- 500K–2M trial signups to convert
- CAC < $700 (30% churn, $2,800 LTV); $675 acceptable CAC, $67.50 per signup
- No inherent virality, inadequate paid ad inventory, ARPA too low for cold calling
- Solution: inbound marketing + exceptional product with high NPS + funnel optimization + OEM distribution
- Inside sales becomes marginally viable; AEs must close ~230 customers/year (one per working day) — brutal

### Deer ($3,300–$33,000 ARPA / ~$10k/yr mid-market)
- 10,000 mid-market customers required
- CAC < $13,000 (15% churn, $57,000 LTV)
- Most rabbit tactics apply; inside sales team now clearly viable
- Channel partners/resellers can receive attractive commissions
- Many SaaS companies transition rabbit→deer by recruiting VP of Sales with inside sales background
- Outbound typically doesn't work; inbound + MQL generation at stable CPL is the real challenge
- 200,000 MQLs needed at 5% MQL-to-win

### Elephants ($33,000–$333,000 ARPA / ~$100k+/yr enterprise)
- Only 1,000 customers needed ($100k < one executive's salary)
- CAC < $130,000 (10% churn, $850,000 LTV)
- Requires field marketing, account-based marketing, relationship-building — heavy upfront capital
- Founders need enterprise sales background; years of dev to achieve PMF
- Workday, Veeva, SuccessFactors, Salesforce as exemplars

### Whales (ARPA > $333,000)
- CAC < $1.3M (7% churn, $12M LTV)
- Clear playbook but requires substantial capital and long pilot cycles

## The Freemium Test (Three Rules)
1. Does your paid plan achieve 80–90% gross margins? (High support costs kill freemium)
2. Does your free plan attract the right audience? (Free users must resemble paying customers)
3. Is your product inherently viral? (Without virality, freemium is less likely to succeed)

## The Critical Upstream Decision

The thesis is structural: **average revenue per account (ARPA) determines the entire GTM machine**, not the other way around. You cannot graft enterprise sales onto a mouse-priced product, and you cannot rely on viral consumer loops for an elephant-priced one. The question is not "what sales tactics should we use" but "what animal are we hunting, and does our ARPA match our distribution channel?"

## VERSO RELEVANCE

### §12 — Positioning + GTM: Channel Choice and Pricing Tier

**[CONFIRMS]** Verso's primary persona (structurally-aware senior/AI engineer at a B2B company) and likely PLG/DX-led motion sits squarely in the **rabbit-to-deer transition zone** ($1k–$10k ARPA). This is the hardest tier in the framework: too expensive for pure viral consumer loops, too cheap for traditional enterprise field sales, requiring inbound + exceptional product + high NPS. This confirms that DX-led organic GTM (Show HN, X build-in-public) is not just a taste preference — it is the *only* scalable channel for this ARPA band. **Verdict: CONFIRMS the DX-led organic GTM decision. Lock it in as structural, not stylistic.**

**[SHARPENS]** The Janz framework sharpens the go/no-go decision on sales motion. Verso should NOT add an enterprise field sales team until ARPA is clearly in the deer range ($10k+/yr per account). Pre-revenue enterprise sales conversations should be explicitly *not* pitched as sales — the Kracov "feedback call" framing from this same batch is the right early substitute. **Verdict: sharpen — sequence matters, don't add sales before the ARPA justifies it.**

**[NEW-IDEA]** The AE productivity math for the rabbit tier is brutal: 230 customers/year = one close per working day. This implies Verso's first monetization pass must be **self-serve and low-touch**, with no manual AE cycle per customer. Any SDK license, paid tier, or team plan must be activatable without a call. This is a product constraint as much as a GTM constraint. **Verdict: adopt-now — make self-serve the default commercial motion.**

**[CONFIRMS]** Janz's empirical finding that 2/3 of unicorns are deer/elephant hunters reinforces the strategic wisdom of *eventually* moving toward team/org-level pricing (deer tier) rather than staying at individual developer pricing (mice/rabbit). But the path from rabbit→deer is through inside sales and channel, not field sales. **Verdict: CONFIRMS the eventual team-plan tier but not the timing.**

### §2 — Non-Goals / Principled Refusal

**[CONFIRMS]** The Janz framework validates one of Verso's principled refusals: do not build an elaborate enterprise sales process pre-revenue. The ARPA doesn't justify it. **Verdict: CONFIRMS. Non-goal explicit in the product strategy.**

### §1 — Bet / Moat / Defensibility

**[SHARPENS]** The 74-unicorn empirical data point is useful for a Verso investor narrative: the deer tier ($3,300–$33,000 ARPA) is where most unicorns live. Verso's framework-to-harness positioning, sold as a team/org capability rather than individual dev tooling, naturally reaches deer-tier pricing once team plans are introduced. The moat argument should mention this trajectory explicitly. **Verdict: sharpen §1 investor narrative.**

### Founder-OS Layer

**[NEW-IDEA]** The framework is the canonical tool for the *upstream* GTM decision. Before any founder-led sales tactics matter, you must know your animal. Verso's founder-OS playbook should include this as Step 0 in the GTM section: "what animal are you hunting, and does your ARPA match your distribution channel?" This prevents the most common early GTM mistake (mismatching sales motion to price point). **Verdict: adopt-now — add to founder-OS index (research/04_bad_guide_full).**
