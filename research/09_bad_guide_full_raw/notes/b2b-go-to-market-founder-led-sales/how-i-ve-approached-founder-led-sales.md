# How I've Approached Founder-Led Sales

**Source:** Alex Kracov (CEO & Co-Founder of Dock; ex-VP of Marketing at Lattice), ~2021  
**URL:** https://www.kracov.co/writing/founder-sales  
**Status:** read_full

---

## Core Argument

Kracov argues that founder-led sales is structurally different from post-PMF sales because the objective is product discovery, not revenue. The early calls should be explicitly positioned as "feedback calls" — the goal is fixing the product, not closing a deal. This framing is also a more honest self-presentation to the prospect (less awkward, more authentic) and gets more meetings because people in the SV ecosystem will give feedback that they won't give a salesperson.

Key thesis: "The biggest thing that makes founder-led sales different is that you're trying to get to product-market fit and have to build a sales process from scratch."

---

## The "Feedback Call" Positioning

- All early calls positioned as "feedback calls": "My main objective was figuring out how to make our product better, not generate revenue."
- The question being answered: what features do we need to build in order to get the customer excited?
- If the prospect seems interested → guide toward action items around using the product
- If they're not a fit → run full product research: ask about their business model, pain points, why they're not a fit
- "This is another reason why it's so important for a founder to do sales themselves. Founders can have a number of calls under this premise, but it gets a lot harder for a sales rep to do this."

---

## The Demo Call Anatomy (25-30 min)

1. **Open (5-10 min):** Quick founding story + the problem being solved. Then 2-3 questions to understand their current business process — use this to *tailor the demo*, not to deliver a scripted pitch.
2. **Demo (10 min):** Screen share, pre-crafted ~10 min product demo with built-in question breaks. "Over time, I crafted a ~10min product demo pitch and had built in breaks to make sure they would feel comfortable asking questions."
3. **Close / Next Steps (5-10 min):** Goal is not purchase — goal is **pilot program**. For non-prospects: use this time for deeper product research.

Key: "When I first started to do demo calls, I had a grand vision of using a pitch deck... But I quickly realized that this didn't work well for our current stage startup. It felt a bit awkward to pull up slides and run through a robotic pitch."

---

## The Pilot as the Goal

- Demo call goal: get the customer to start a pilot program, not buy
- "Instead of a full-company rollout, the customers can start to use the product with a couple users to make sure it works and provides value."
- "You want to have a very clear goal / action item. Importantly, this doesn't just mean buying your product."
- Pilot reduces customer risk, reveals product gaps, and creates a natural success/failure event to advance the sale

## Doing Things That Don't Scale

Kracov's team built Dock spaces for prospects manually at the end of every demo call:
> "In terms of next steps, I'd be happy to set up a template to help you get started. Can you send over what you typically share with customers? Forward your demo follow up email, marketing collateral, pitch decks, etc. and we'll turn it into a Dock space."

This "dogfood" tactic served two purposes: (1) removed the friction of getting started, (2) demonstrated the product's value in the prospect's actual workflow. The meta insight: **using your own product during the sales process is the most powerful demo you can run.**

## Pattern Recognition Across Calls

Questions Kracov was synthesizing across calls in real-time:
- Are we building the right overall product? Is our vision correct?
- What features do we need to build? What's the order?
- Am I talking to the right person? Is this our ICP?
- What do I say to explain what we're building? What words resonate with different ICPs?
- What does genuine enthusiasm look like vs. polite skepticism?

The signal he was optimizing for: "the level of enthusiasm the person I was talking to had for the idea and our vision." Enthusiastic early users = ICP. The frequency of enthusiasm increasing over time was the PMF signal.

## Operational Mechanics

**Email templates (Mixmax):**
- Inbound demo request: schedule the call
- Demo follow-up V1: prospect wants to move forward — pilot action items and onboarding steps
- Demo follow-up V2: prospect still deciding — softer language, "whenever you're ready"
- Demo follow-up V3: not interested — thank you + "we'll stay in touch as the product evolves"

**Calendar management:** Timeboxed demo calls to Tue/Wed/Thu afternoons to preserve focus blocks for product/writing. Calendly for scheduling.

**CRM (Airtable):** Deliberately minimal — Accounts / Contacts / Opportunities / Inbound Leads. "I'm optimizing for speed and my own mental health over the perfect sales data funnel." Reviews opportunity pipeline daily + weekly team review.

**Lead sources:**
- Kracov.co newsletter email list
- Gmail contacts scraped and emailed individually
- Twitter and LinkedIn posts (described as "hugely helpful")
- VC and friend introductions
- Beta launch day: surge of inbound demo requests

## The Observation About Network-Building

"I always knew I wanted to be a founder, so I spent years slowly building my network to help folks with random marketing advice, product feedback, etc. I leveraged a lot of these relationships in order to get the ball moving with founder-led sales. If you want to be a founder, take a bunch of other calls with folks to help them out and you'll have a huge network of folks to reach out to when the time comes."

## VERSO RELEVANCE

### §12 — GTM / Positioning

**[CONFIRMS]** The "feedback call" framing is the canonical early-stage implementation of the DX-led organic GTM already established for Verso. Verso's first conversations with senior engineers / AI engineers at companies should NOT be framed as "let me show you our framework" — they should be "we're building a harness and want to understand how your team handles context and reliability." This is more honest and generates better signal. **Verdict: CONFIRMS — but should be made explicit in Verso's founder-OS playbook as a named tactic.**

**[NEW-IDEA]** Kracov's observation about the pitch deck failing early-stage is directly relevant to Verso's demo strategy. The 3-line side-by-side (the demo-able wedge already established) should NOT be wrapped in slides — it should be run live, in their terminal or their codebase, with pauses for questions. The meta-insight: showing the one-flag durable diff in a GitHub repo they already use is the Verso equivalent of building a Dock space from their own collateral. **Verdict: adopt-now — the demo must happen in the prospect's context, not in a prepared Notion doc.**

**[SHARPENS]** Kracov's three-bucket email follow-up (V1 = ready to move, V2 = still deciding, V3 = not interested) is a precise template Verso can adopt verbatim with domain substitution. The V3 email ("we'll stay in touch as the product evolves") is particularly important: it keeps the door open and treats the feedback-only conversation as a long-term relationship, not a failed sale. **Verdict: sharpen — encode this three-bucket template in founder-OS playbook.**

**[NEW-IDEA]** The pilot-as-goal framing maps directly to Verso's trial wedge. The equivalent for Verso is not "buy a license" but "run your next agent with Verso's harness and see what the eval report looks like." The pilot success metric is a measurable harness lift (analogous to the +29pp v0 or Sierra 61%/35% data points already established). Make the pilot success condition explicit before the pilot starts. **Verdict: adopt-now — define Verso's pilot success metric and bake it into every early conversation.**

### §7 / Demo-able Wedge

**[SHARPENS]** "Using your own product during the sales process is the most powerful demo you can run." For Verso, this means the founder should run every Verso workflow (planning, eval loop, HITL promotion) using Verso itself. The GitHub issues, the planning notes, the eval reports — all should be Verso artifacts. This is not just taste — it is the demonstration that the harness is production-worthy. **Verdict: sharpen §7 — dogfood-first is a demo asset, not just an internal principle.**

### Founder-OS Layer

**[NEW-IDEA]** The deliberate minimal CRM philosophy ("optimizing for speed and my own mental health over the perfect sales data funnel") is an important operating principle for Verso pre-revenue. Over-engineered CRM at this stage is a form of productive procrastination. Use a simple Airtable or even a Notion table: Accounts / Contacts / Opportunities / Inbound. Review daily. **Verdict: adopt-now — encode in founder-OS as the pre-revenue CRM policy.**

**[CONFIRMS]** Calendar batching (demo calls Tue/Wed/Thu afternoons only) is the correct operating structure for a founder who also needs to build product. Verso's founder should protect at minimum 2 full days per week for product/coding, untouched by sales calls. **Verdict: CONFIRMS — encode in founder-OS operating calendar.**

**[SHARPENS]** The enthusiasm-detection signal ("the level of enthusiasm the person I was talking to had for the idea and our vision" as the ICP proxy) is sharpened for Verso's context. The equivalent enthusiasm signal is: does the engineer start asking "can I do X with this?" — that is the moment they've mentally adopted the framework. Track this as a qualitative leading indicator before any revenue metric. **Verdict: sharpen — define the Verso-specific enthusiasm signal in the founder-OS playbook.**
