## Core Argument

Going from zero to many users is a craft problem, not a marketing problem. Cheung's organizing thesis: the early loop is **build → talk → build**, and most founders skip the "talk" step or insert it too late. The lecture is a practitioner's playbook, structured around sequential stages: before you write code, building the MVP, getting the first users, gathering feedback, and then growth.

---

## Key Frameworks

### 1. Pre-Build: Deep Industry Embedding

Before writing a line of code, become the user:
- "If your idea is related to restaurants, become a waiter."
- Cheung took a cleaning job before building Homejoy — not for content marketing, but to surface operational inefficiencies competitors couldn't see from the outside.
- Read S-1 filings, earnings calls, and competitor research exhaustively.

**Lesson:** Founders who skip this build for a customer they've imagined, not a real one.

### 2. Minimal Viable Product = Minimal *Viable*

The emphasis is on *viable*, not minimal. Many founders ship something so stripped that it doesn't actually solve the core problem. You need to thread the needle: "minimum viable product" requires the core job-to-be-done to actually be done.

One-liner positioning follows the same discipline. Homejoy's multi-paragraph description failed; "get your place cleaned for $20 an hour" succeeded. Functional benefits before emotional branding.

### 3. Stealth Mode Is a Trap

"Unless you are building something that requires tens of millions of dollars just to start up there is really no point in waiting." TechCrunch launches without user-tested products produce traffic spikes with zero retention: "no one sticks around because you didn't get that initial user feedback."

### 4. First Users: The Sourcing Ladder

1. Yourself, co-founders, family, friends
2. Online communities (Hacker News, niche forums)
3. Local mailing lists, niche influencers
4. Cold outreach, press

Creative physical tactics also work (Homejoy distributed frozen water bottles at street fairs to drive initial bookings in a high-traffic context).

### 5. Feedback Mechanics: Interviews Beat Surveys

- Surveys only capture extremes — lovers and haters. Interviews reveal the middle, which is where friction lives.
- Direct phone/email support lines are essential: they force founders to hear bad news in real time.
- **The honesty curve**: "Your mom won't criticize your free product; strangers paying money provide brutal honesty." Paying users are your best signal source.
- Track quantitatively: retention cohorts, NPS, star ratings — but qualitative interviews are primary.

### 6. The Frankenstein Trap / Feature Creep

Don't build every feature users request. Identify the *underlying problem* driving the request. "Temporary brokenness beats permanent paralysis" — ship imperfect, functional solutions. Optimize for your current scale (10 users, not 10 million). Manual processes precede automation.

**"13 iterations":** Homejoy required 13 fully-built product iterations before finding product-market fit. This is not a failure story — it's the normal timeline.

### 7. Three Growth Types

**Sticky (Retention):**
Measure CLV and retention cohorts. Plot curves: healthy curves flatten as a core segment stabilizes; unhealthy curves keep declining. Repeat users generate cumulative revenue.

**Viral:**
Exceptional UX + structured referral mechanics. Reveal referral links at high-engagement moments. Test incentive structures ($10/$10 vs $25/$25). Optimize conversion flow.

**Paid:**
CLV > CAC. Segment by cohort — different markets have different CLVs. Tailor ad spend accordingly. Payback period: ≤3 months for risk-averse, ≤12 months maximum. Beyond 12 months = unsustainable debt cycle.

### 8. One Channel at a Time

"Focus exclusively on one growth strategy for a full week. If it works, iterate until saturation. If it fails, move on." This prevents diluted effort.

### 9. Overcoming Switching Costs

Create one obvious moment of differentiation, not 50 incremental ones. Cheung's example: existing users who couldn't book their regular cleaner on short notice switched to Homejoy for next-day availability. One clear wedge beats a feature list.

### 10. Pivot Criteria

Real signal for pivoting: three to four weeks of zero growth despite full execution. Growth curves have lumps — distinguish a temporary lull (resolves in 2-3 weeks) from a structural stall. Evaluate on three dimensions: growth stalling, retention collapsing, poor unit economics.

## Key Quotes

- "One or two days straight per week focused work outperforms two hours here and there every single day."
- "Unless you are building something that requires tens of millions of dollars just to start up there is really no point in waiting."
- "No one sticks around because you didn't get that initial user feedback."
- "Temporary brokenness beats permanent paralysis."
- "Who you talk to is just as important as what questions you ask and what you pull away from it."

## VERSO RELEVANCE

### §12 Positioning / GTM — cold-start and wedge

**[SHARPENS]** The "one channel at a time" discipline maps directly onto Verso's cold-start strategy. At Stage-1 (~5 vocal production leads), diluted effort across multiple channels is fatal. The instruction to saturate one channel before moving is a concrete operating principle for the organic/DX-led GTM.

**[SHARPENS]** "Interviews beat surveys" is an operational mandate for Verso's early user research. The structurally-aware senior engineer persona — the primary target — will not fill in a form, but will talk if the product has surprised them. Phone/async conversations, not NPS surveys, are the signal source at scale-0.

**[CONFIRMS]** The honesty-curve framing (paying users = brutal truth, free users = polite noise) confirms the DX-led, "earn your first users" GTM approach over free tiers or vague community noise. This specifically validates prioritizing paid/committed early adopters over raw GitHub stars.

### §7 Demo-able Wedge

**[SHARPENS]** The "one differentiator beats 50 improvements" insight sharpens the wedge design rule. Verso's demo should demonstrate ONE moment where the seam matters (promote-in-place), not list every feature. The switching-cost moment must be obvious and singular.

**[NEW-IDEA — founder_os]** The "concentrated block" operating principle (one or two full days > fragmented hours) is a VERSO builder operating principle. Deep context-switching cost matters for framework builders: if you're not immersed in agent loop behavior for a sustained stretch, you miss the subtle failure modes that only emerge at depth.

### §1 Bet / Moat

**[CONFIRMS]** The 13-iteration path to PMF normalizes structural iteration without crisis. Verso's "non-goals are evidence-updatable" principle is consistent with Cheung's pivot criteria: growth stalling + retention collapse + bad unit economics = structural signal, not just a rough week.

- One channel at a time → operational rule for Verso's cold-start Stage-1. Ship.
