## What Baxley Actually Argues

### Core Thesis
Software is a medium — on the same footing as film, music, and books — not a functional utility. Every piece of software produces an emotional response: the user either feels empowered or confused, their world gets bigger or smaller. Designers therefore have a **moral obligation** to be intentional about which emotion they produce. Great product design requires, in order: (1) a clear vision, (2) design tenants (decision-making rules, not platitudes), (3) small Beatles-sized creative teams, and (4) treating design as phase-zero of engineering, not a downstream function.

---

### Key Frameworks and Mental Models

**1. Software as medium — the Northern California thesis.**
The origin of Silicon Valley was a small group at Stanford who saw software as a new form of media on par with movies, music, and books. That framing is the right one. Filmmakers go to films, comedians go to comedy clubs — they study the medium. Software designers rarely go watch people use software. Every interaction a user has evokes an emotion. Design's job: be intentional about that emotion. Ask first: "What emotion do we want this person to have?" Then build the visual and interaction to produce it.

**2. Design tenants vs. principles — the critical distinction.**
Principles are platitudes: simple, clear, beautiful, fast. Nobody argues the opposite, so they generate no decisions. **Tenants are decision-making rules that close recurring debates permanently.** Steve Jobs and Keynote: *"Difficult to make ugly presentations, cinematic quality transitions, optimize for innovation over PowerPoint compatibility."* That last tenant — optimize for innovation over PowerPoint compatibility — if Jobs hadn't decided it explicitly, the team would have debated it for a decade. Tenants = the paper-vs-plastic decisions you make once and follow forever.

Baxley's ThoughtSpot tenants (concrete example):
1. Documentation is a failure state — if users need to read instructions, the design failed.
2. Every interaction starts simple; users opt into complexity.
3. The entire product should look and feel like it came from a single mind.

**3. The Beatles principle — team size is a quality variable.**
*"You get the Beatles with four people. You don't get the Beatles with eight people, and you certainly don't get it with 24 people."* Apple ran the App Store redesign with **6 designers** for a store in 30+ countries, 12,500 store instances, and billions in revenue. Any other company would have had 60+. Apple operates with much smaller creative staff because it has clarity of vision. Creative coherence requires small teams. Scale is where it dies.

**4. Design must report to engineering.**
*"That's how it worked at Apple the whole time under Steve. Design always reported to engineering."* When design reports to product, engineering gets left out until the end, hasn't been brought along, and doesn't know how to extrapolate specs into what should ship. Design as "phase zero of the engineering process" keeps creative and implementation tightly connected from concept through delivery. The org chart is a design quality lever.

**5. Wait as long as possible to draw a picture — the primal mark.**
"Primal mark" concept from art: the first mark on the canvas makes everything after it a response to that mark. As soon as you show a realistic mockup, everyone collapses onto it and forgets the broader possibility space. GenAI tools that generate UIs from prompts take your first-order thinking and output it back at you — they narrow the possibility space to what already exists. Stay conceptual and conversational until the idea is genuinely ready to be expressed visually. Quote: *"There's nothing worse than a brilliant image of a fuzzy concept."* (Ansel Adams, cited by Baxley.)

**6. Metrics as consequence, not driver.**
*"I've never seen a product be successful that used metrics as a driver. I've seen a lot of companies be really successful seeing metrics as a consequence and a way to evaluate the quality of their decisions."* Metrics tell you how the decisions are working; they don't tell you what to decide. The substitution of "does it convert?" for "does it feel right?" is the failure mode (same observation as the Linear/Karri Saarinen thesis).

**7. The moral obligation frame.**
*"People don't want to try to figure out how to navigate our login screens or go through our onboarding process. They just wanna get home and spend time with their families and pet their dogs and have a nice dinner."* Almost everyone in a modern economy has hundreds of interactions per day with phones and computers. Designers have an obligation to put emotional energy back into those interactions — not to frustrate people, not to exploit their cognitive load. *"We have an obligation as product people to put emotional energy back into people's lives."*

**8. Vision drives efficiency — the Disney/Google model.**
Companies with clear organizational vision (Disney: "happiest place on earth"; Google: "organizing all the world's information") operate more efficiently with smaller teams because everyone knows what to build. Yahoo's lack of a clear vision created confusion that required more headcount to navigate. Vision is not a strategy statement — it is an operational efficiency lever. *"Apple itself"* was Jobs's proudest creation, not any individual product — because the culture could sustain and regenerate quality.

**9. Design-led ≠ designer-led.**
*"Saying a company is design-led does not mean it's designer-led. I've never seen somebody graft it on after the fact. It's there at the beginning in the root DNA or it doesn't exist."* Design culture cannot be installed — it must be founding DNA. This is the assessment criterion for joining or building a company.

**10. Developing design intuition.**
Watch people use software — NOT your own product (you have positive bias). Watch them use adjacent products, even completely unrelated ones. Baxley's method: for a checkout redesign, had subjects navigate eBay, Williams-Sonoma, and Amazon — learned things he'd never have learned from watching their own product. *"We work in a medium. Software is a medium. We need to understand our medium."* The implication: grocery self-checkouts, parking meters, banking apps — all valid study objects.

## VERSO RELEVANCE

### [SHARPENS] §12 / positioning — Design tenants as a VERSO positioning instrument
**Finding:** The tenants-vs-principles distinction is directly deployable in VERSO's external communication. VERSO's non-goals/principled-refusals are currently framed as "we don't do X." Baxley's model shows they should be framed as **tenants** — permanent decision rules that close recurring debates. Example conversion: "we don't force the graph" (principle-style, reactive) → "harness is ambient, loop is yours: we will never own your control flow" (tenant-style, proactive, closes the debate). Tenants that could be stated for VERSO: (1) the agent loop is not the product, (2) structural durability must not require a rewrite, (3) context density is a first-class primitive, not an afterthought.  
**VERSO PRD section:** §12 positioning, §2 non-goals.

### [CONFIRMS] product_taste — Small team as quality precondition, not constraint
**Finding:** The Beatles principle CONFIRMS VERSO's dogfood-first / personal-infra operating stance. A small team (even 1-2) holding the whole product in one head is not a resource limitation — it is the structural condition for creative coherence. This CONFIRMS: resisting premature team scaling is a quality strategy, not a hiring shortage.  
**VERSO PRD section:** founder_os / product_taste.

### [NEW-IDEA] §7 / product_taste — "Wait as long as possible to draw a picture" as API design principle
**Finding:** Baxley's primal-mark concept maps to a specific VERSO product risk: defining the API surface (the "picture") before the right abstraction is found. Every early API choice collapses the possibility space. The GenAI-tools-narrow-to-existing-solutions observation is a warning about using LLM-assisted API design: it outputs first-order thinking back. VERSO's seam design (tactical/durable split, one-flag promotion) survived because it was held in conceptual form longer before being expressed as code. **New idea:** explicitly name "stay conceptual longer" as a VERSO design discipline — resist premature API crystallization even under user demand for stability.  
**Tag:** NEW-IDEA  
**VERSO PRD section:** §7 demo-able wedge / product_taste.

### [SHARPENS] founder_os — Vision-as-operational-efficiency, not strategy document
**Finding:** Baxley's point that vision clarity directly reduces headcount and debate overhead is a sharper version of the "founder stays in the details" thesis. For VERSO: the product vision ("harness is the seam, loop is yours, structural durability by one flag") needs to be clear enough to function as an operational filter — closing recurring feature requests and debates without requiring a meeting. This is the VERSO equivalent of Jobs's "optimize for innovation over PowerPoint compatibility" tenant.
