## Faithful Digest

A short essay (under 500 words) by Karri Saarinen — later co-founder of Linear — written thirteen years before the Linear redesign piece. Its brevity is load-bearing: it is an argument that fits in memory, which is the kind of argument that changes behavior.

### The Structural Argument

Saarinen's claim is built from Jiro Ono's own words about taste development:

> "In order to make delicious food, you must eat delicious food. You need to develop a palate capable of discerning good and bad. Without a good taste, you can't make good food. If your sense of taste is lower than that of customers, how will you impress them?"

This is not about technique. It is about the perceptual prerequisite for quality production. You cannot output taste you have not developed the capacity to recognize. The constraint is epistemic before it is executional.

### The Three Intellectual Ancestors Saarinen Invokes

1. **Paul Graham** — "Taste for Makers" (the taste-as-skill thesis applied to code/product)
2. **Steve Jobs** — "Great products are triumphs of taste"
3. **John Gruber** — "Auteur Theory of Design" (the single coherent aesthetic sensibility thesis)

These three references form a canonical citation cluster for the taste-in-tech argument. They are predictably present but their co-citation signals Saarinen's intellectual formation — and they are all in circulation in the builder community Verso needs to reach.

### How Taste Is Developed

Saarinen does not claim a definitive answer but names cultural consumption as the mechanism: "reading literature, viewing art, exploring application design, collecting inspiration, consuming quality food, visiting beautiful locations, and surrounding oneself with well-designed objects." He explicitly notes uncertainty about which activities matter most, acknowledging "learning occurs through exposure to both excellence and mediocrity."

The argument is not prescriptive. It is honest: "I lack definitive answers." This intellectual honesty register is the same register Verso has committed to — decade not year, explain it without AI, no magic box.

### The Jiro Collaborator Detail

Saarinen notes Jiro's fish vendor and rice supplier work with him "not for financial gain but from shared commitment to excellence and desire to partner with the best." The network of excellence is self-selecting. The vendors and the chef share a standard, and that shared standard is why the collaboration works. This is a different distribution logic than marketing — it is quality as a selection mechanism for collaborators and customers.

### What Saarinen Is Actually Arguing for Founders/Builders

The core claim, translated: the bottleneck in making something great is not execution skill, it is perceptual capability — the ability to recognize the difference between good and bad before committing to a direction. If you cannot taste the difference, you cannot course-correct toward the north star.

The practical implication: builders must deliberately invest in consuming excellent work, not just in shipping work.

## VERSO RELEVANCE

### [CONFIRMS] §7 / §12 — Taste as the non-copyable product surface

The Jiro argument confirms that taste is a learnable but accreted capability — not a checklist, not a template. This supports Verso's positioning: the harness is taste-encoded in invariants, and invariants cannot be copied without the perceptual history that produced them. This is a flavor of moat argument: the specific shape of Verso's invariants is downstream of the founders' palate, which is not transferable.

**Verdict:** confirm.

### [SHARPENS] §12 Positioning / intellectual honesty register

Saarinen explicitly admits "I lack definitive answers" while making a strong claim. This is precisely the register Verso has committed to ("decade not year," "explain it without AI"). The Jiro essay is evidence that this register is associated with high-taste builders in Verso's target community — it reads as credible rather than weak.

**Verdict:** confirm — reinforces the register, no action needed.

### [NEW-IDEA] §12 GTM / community formation — quality as collaborator selection

The fish vendor / rice supplier dynamic describes a distribution mechanism Verso has not yet named: when you build at a recognizable quality standard, the right collaborators (early adopters, contributors, integration partners) self-select toward you. This is different from demand generation — it is signal-as-filter. Verso's equivalent: the kinds of engineers who read the 3-line diff and immediately understand the seam are self-selecting. The product is a quality beacon, not just a utility.

**Verdict:** adopt-now — surfaces the "become hunted" mechanism at the level of taste. Use in the §12 distribution/leverage section.

### [SHARPENS] Founder-OS — deliberate taste investment as operating practice

The essay argues for scheduled cultural consumption as a founder operating habit. For Verso's founder-OS layer: deliberately consuming excellent agent frameworks (even competitors), excellent CLIs, excellent developer tools — not to copy them but to build the perceptual baseline for knowing when Verso is and is not good. This is analogous to Saarinen's daily Figma screen generation during parental leave.

**Verdict:** lock-defer — file under founder-OS / quarterly practices.

### [CONFIRMS] §2 Non-goals — avoiding "taste of customers lower than yours" dynamic in reverse

Jiro's warning is directional: your taste floor must exceed your customers' taste floor or you cannot impress them. The inverse failure mode for Verso is building for an imaginary sophisticated user when the actual early adopter population is not yet that sophisticated. The PMF section (Superhuman source) provides the counter-mechanism — but Jiro provides the principle of why building for the real high-expectation customer matters.

**Verdict:** confirm — pair with Superhuman PMF note.
