## SECTION founder-mind — "The Founder Mind (high-agency, top-1%)" (lines 81–197)

**[KNOWN] The shift (line 85):** "The 90% treat founding as an execution problem... The 1% treat it as a perception and response problem. Problem selection dominates execution skill, the best ideas are noticed not brainstormed, and the thing that actually separates outliers is how you respond to novel obstacles in real time." Also: "refusing to perform ambition you don't feel."

### Principle: Ideas are noticed by a prepared mind, not generated in a brainstorm (line 89–90)
**[KNOWN]** "The set of plausible-sounding ideas is huge; the set of good ones is tiny, and they look identical from outside. Only someone who lived in a domain for years can tell them apart. Gates heard about the Altair like thousands did, but he'd spent years on interpreters. **Live in the future, build what's missing.**" ← the "noticing ideas by living-in-the-future" anchor.

### Principle: The schlep IS the business (line 92–93)
**[KNOWN]** "Your unconscious filters out the best ideas before you consciously see them, because the work looks tedious. Stripe is the case: every developer knew payments were painful, nobody built it because the bank deals and fraud and compliance got filtered out pre-consideration. Then PayPal's fraud detection became the moat (**Levchin: 'a security company posing as a transaction processor'**). The bypass question: **what problem do I wish someone else would solve for me?**" ← the "do-the-schlep-as-moat" anchor.

### Principle: A real opportunity has three parts, and most people stop at two (line 95–96)
**[KNOWN]** "Conventional wisdom + a contrarian truth + a tech enabler that makes the truth doable NOW. Everyone with a contrarian take stops at part two. The timing check is where theses die. The enabler must be recent enough that incumbents haven't adopted it and mature enough for production. **If your insight leads you to the same architecture as incumbents, it isn't contrarian enough.**" ← Thiel's three-part contrarian-insight structure (also restated at line 1469 summary table and line 673 in revolutionary section).

### Principle: The founder gene is resourcefulness (line 98–99)
**[KNOWN]** "**Graham's two-word definition: relentlessly resourceful, the opposite of hapless.** Hapless is passivity in the face of a novel obstacle. Resourceful means you have your way with the world instead of the world having its way with you. This is the one trait that predicts outcomes, and it's a response pattern you can train: when a wall appears, the default move is to go around it, not to file a ticket and wait." ← PG relentlessly-resourceful anchor. Essay: `Relentlessly Resourceful` (https://paulgraham.com/relres.html, `MUST`).

### Principle: Earnestness is the moat you can't fake at scale (line 101–102)
**[KNOWN]** "Genuine care about the problem (not the outcome, not the valuation) is both the strongest motivator and a real moat, because it can't be faked across thousands of small decisions. **Graham's formula: formidable + earnest = unstoppable.** The 90% perform ambition. Stop performing it and start having it." ← "earnestness that can't be faked" anchor. Essay: `Earnestness` (https://paulgraham.com/earnest.html, `STRONG`).
**[KNOWN] Garry Tan / YC framing (line 1354, fundraising section):** "Garry Tan says the rare signal YC funds is earnestness: people who discovered something real by building, not founders selling a story."
**[KNOWN] Alexandr Wang operationalization (line 148):** "'you are the upper bound for how much anyone in your company will care; too much is the right amount.'" (essay *Do Too Much*)

### Principle: Inaction is a hidden form of risk (line 104–105)
**[KNOWN]** "**Altman's line: 'inaction is a particularly insidious type of risk' because it feels safe.** The corollary that should reorder your week: it's easier to do a hard thing that really matters than an easy thing that doesn't. **Plan in decades, execute in weeks.**" Essay: `What I Wish Someone Had Told Me` (https://blog.samaltman.com/what-i-wish-someone-had-told-me).

## SECTION revolutionary-building — "Building Revolutionary Products (primitives, not wrappers)" (lines 649–808)

**[KNOWN] The shift (line 653):** "The 90% build the product first and treat the hard infrastructure as a cost to minimize. **The 1% build the primitive first and let the product fall out of it as a consequence.** Figma spent ~4 years on a WebGL renderer and a multiplayer protocol before the design tool existed. **If your architecture is the same as the incumbent's plus an LLM call, you built a faster horse with a chat box.**" ← "primitives-not-wrappers / revolutions-not-increments" anchor.

### Principle: Build the primitive; the product is its consequence (line 657–658)
**[KNOWN]** "Perplexity built the retrieval+synthesis pipeline, not the chat UI. Cursor built speculative editing, not the IDE chrome. Linear built the sync engine, not the kanban board. **You have a primitive when you can state it in one sentence**... **Name it in one sentence or you don't have it yet.**"

### Principle: Apply the wrapper test to your own roadmap (line 660–661)
**[KNOWN]** "**Swap the underlying model out in your head. If the product is basically the same, you built a wrapper.** ... does your product have architectural dependencies that would take a well-funded competitor months to rebuild from scratch? Sequoia's retention benchmark... **Real products hit 60–85% DAU/MAU; the average AI app sat near 14%.** **If a base-model upgrade *helps* you instead of *killing* you, you're on the right side of this line.**" [INFERRED: directly VALIDATES Verso's "byte-identical-when-unused / additive / model-swap via init_model + agent.override" — Verso is built to be model-agnostic so an upgrade helps.]

### Principle: Own the layer that sets your quality ceiling (line 663–664)
**[KNOWN]** "If your core primitive depends on a third-party service, that service controls how good you're allowed to be. Perplexity started on the Bing API, hit the ceiling, and built its own 200B-URL index. **The payoff was only 1.4% URL overlap with competitors on identical queries.** Cursor forked VS Code because the extension API made the speculative-edit primitive impossible. **The test: remove the third-party dependency on paper. If your core value proposition dies, you don't control your destiny.**" [INFERRED: Verso's native @tool-is-THE-path + ANY OpenAI-compatible base_url is exactly this "don't be captive to one provider" stance.]

### Principle: The decisive decision usually looks irrational at the time (line 666–667)
**[KNOWN]** "Every revolutionary product made a call that looked wrong when they made it. StackBlitz spent ~7 years on WebContainers before AI needed them, then shipped Bolt.new to **$40M ARR in 6 months with 15 engineers**. Midjourney took no VC and ran Discord-only, hit **$500M ARR with ~100 people**. Discord picked Elixir/Erlang in 2015 when it was niche. **the irrational-looking bet *was* the structural moat.**"

### Principle: Composite architecture beats both wrappers and your own foundation model (line 669–670)
**[KNOWN]** "The middle path is where the wins are. **Vercel's v0 took Claude Sonnet's 64.71% error-free rate to 93.87% with engineering, not model training: RAG context injection, real-time stream-fixing during generation, deterministic AST fixes, a fine-tuned AutoFixer.** **Malte Ubl's rule: don't train your own foundation models if you want to iterate fast; use a composite architecture to stay flexible.** **The compounding moat is the harness around the model, not the model itself. It survives every frontier release instead of being obsoleted by it.**" [INFERRED: this is the strongest single validation of Verso's whole thesis — Verso IS the harness, not the model; NON-GOAL "not the model/kernel" is exactly Ubl's rule.]

### Principle: Pick the boundary where convention meets a new enabler (line 672–673)
**[KNOWN]** "Thiel's contrarian insight has three parts... the conventional wisdom, the truth you see, and the *technology enabler that makes it actionable now*. Too far into the frontier and nobody buys yet; too close to convention and you have no defensible edge. **If your insight leads to the same architecture as the incumbents, it isn't contrarian enough.** Karpathy's Software 3.0 framing is the substrate: the LLM is the new kind of computer, and the whole stack from interface to orchestration is up for a redesign, not a patch."
