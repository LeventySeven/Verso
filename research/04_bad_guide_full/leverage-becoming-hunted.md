## How to Get Rich (without getting lucky) — https://nav.al/rich — read — MUST

**Thesis:** Wealth (assets that earn while you sleep) is not money or status. The path: specific knowledge + accountability + permissionless leverage, compounded over time. Deterministic, not lucky.

**Core framework — Four Types of Leverage:**
1. **Labor** — hiring people; permissioned, zero-sum, declining relevance
2. **Capital** — using money to amplify; permissioned (need institutional acceptance)
3. **Code** — software that scales without additional labor; permissionless
4. **Media** — content, writing, podcasts; permissionless, keeps working at 3am

Permissionless = you need no one's approval. Code and media are the only two. A Rust framework + public writing = the modern fortune stack.

**Specific Knowledge framework:**
- Cannot be mass-taught; built from genuine curiosity and obsession
- Feels like play to you, looks like work to others
- Three sources: DNA/childhood learning (soft skills), brand-new domains (nobody knows yet), on-the-job pattern matching
- Scott Adams principle: top 25% at three different things compounds better than top 1% at one
- "Escape competition through authenticity — no one can compete with you on being you"
- Compounds: someone 90% correct in a leveraged field earns 100x more than someone 80% correct

**Wealth vs. status:** Wealth is positive-sum (everyone can build it); status is zero-sum. Status-players attack wealth-builders because wealth threatens their ranking. Most attacks on ambitious people come from status-seekers.

**Four types of luck:**
1. Blind luck (uncontrollable)
2. Luck from hustling (create surface area)
3. Luck from preparation (skill lets you recognize what others miss)
4. Luck from character — "Build your character in a way that luck becomes deterministic." Your reputation attracts specific opportunities only you can see.
- "In 1,000 parallel universes, you want to be wealthy in 999 of them." Make it deterministic.

**Builder + Seller model:** Best structure is one world-class builder + one world-class seller. "If you can do both, you will be unstoppable." "I'd rather teach an engineer marketing than a marketer engineering."

**Don't rent out your time:** Inputs match outputs directly (hourly), you're replaceable, you stop earning when you stop working. Sleep = $0.

**Internet changes everything:** "The internet has massively broadened the possible space of careers." Connects every human to every other human. 50,000 passionate people in your micro-niche × the internet = viable business. PewDiePie has 3x the distribution of cable news networks.

**Key quotes:**
- "Wealth is assets that earn while you sleep"
- "You won't get rich renting out your time"
- "Escape competition through authenticity"
- "The most dangerous things are heroin and a monthly salary"
- "All returns in life come from compound interest"
- "No one can compete with you on being you"
- "People living far below their means enjoy a freedom that people busy upgrading their lifestyles just can't fathom"

**Why in guide:** The definitive taxonomy of leverage. Establishes that code + media is the only permissionless stack — the operating model for everything else in this section.

## Most Human Wins — https://www.notboring.co/p/most-human-wins — read — MUST

**Thesis:** AI commoditizing cognitive execution is not a threat — it is the same pattern as all prior progress. Cheaper execution makes scarcer human vision more valuable. The moat is not speed of output; it is taste, judgment, and embodied reputation.

**Core framework — "Neti Neti" Economics:**
- Strip away what you're not to reveal what you are.
- "I am not a farmer... not a heavy object carrier... not a computer." Each commoditization clarifies what remains distinctly human.
- "The history of progress is the continued commoditization of once-scarce things."
- Commoditization is the mechanism of compounding, not a threat to it.

**The Einstein-Grossmann inversion:**
- 1907-1912: Einstein had gravitational intuition, lacked the math. Grossmann had Riemannian geometry and tensor calculus — pure academic work.
- Together they produced General Relativity. Einstein "commoditized" Grossmann's labor.
- Modern parallel: o1 Pro can now do Grossmann's job. "Marcel Grossmann is now included in a $200/month subscription."
- The inversion: **"The cheaper Grossmann becomes, the more valuable Einstein becomes."**
- You can outsource execution; you cannot outsource the "happiest thought" — the vision that identifies which problem matters.

**What gets commoditized:**
- Writing, speaking, science, mathematics, code
- "Things that were once hard-won outputs are becoming readily available inputs"
- OpenAI o3: 92.3% on AIME math competition (superhuman)

**What remains uniquely human:**
1. **Intuition / Vision** — identifying which problem matters
2. **Strategy** — recognizing which commodities to combine and how
3. **Authenticity** — irreducible personal combination of experience and perspective

**Strategic response (McCormick's own method):**
- Reject deadline-driven polished mediocrity (withdrew from fixed sponsor schedule)
- Experiment freely with new inputs (AI for editing, proofs, feedback)
- Seek the irreplaceable contribution: "There is one essay that is the best essay only I could write"
- Maximize novel human experience — frame this as expanding what humans can accomplish

**Rhodium vs. Chlorine spectrum:** Rare commodities stay valuable; abundant ones become inputs. Rhodium: $20,880/oz. Chlorine: 8 cents/kg. Know which you are.

**Key quotes:**
- "Humans are not being commoditized. Certain things that we thought only we could do are."
- "The biggest beneficiaries are those who recognize what's becoming commoditized and what becomes possible as a result."
- "Don't let the machines get you down. Make them lift you up."

**Why in guide:** The 2025 positioning brief for not becoming a wrapper. As execution gets cheaper, the moat migrates fully to taste and judgment — exactly what is NOT in Claude Code or Codex.

## 1,000 True Fans — https://kk.org/thetechnium/1000-true-fans/ — read — MUST

**Thesis:** You don't need millions of fans to make a living as a creator. You need ~1,000 who will buy anything you produce. The math works at a scale achievable without fame.

**Exact definition of a True Fan:**
"A True Fan is defined as someone who will purchase anything and everything you produce. They will drive 200 miles to see you sing. They will buy the super deluxe re-issued hi-res box set even though they have the low-res version." They have Google Alerts set for your name, bookmark your eBay listings, attend your openings.

**The math:**
- 1,000 true fans × $100/year average spend = $100,000/year
- "Assume conservatively your True Fans will each spend one day's wages per year"
- Adjustable: $50/fan = need 2,000; $200/fan = need only 500
- For duets/partners, scale proportionally — but linearly, not exponentially
- Successful Kickstarter campaigns average only 241 funders — far below 1,000

**Why 1,000 and not more:**
- "You might even be able to remember a thousand names"
- "If you added one new true fan per day, it'd only take a few years"
- Not a binary choice — works alongside mainstream channels
- Variable by medium: maybe 500 for a painter, 5,000 for a filmmaker

**True fans vs. casual fans:**
- Casual fans expand income by ~50% beyond true fan revenue
- True fans are the chief marketing force: their enthusiasm converts casual fans
- Direct fan relationship (they pay you directly) is essential — every intermediary layer inflates the number needed

**The middle class of creators argument:**
- Starvation or stardom is a false binary
- "A much saner destination" — make a living surrounded by True Fans, not fad
- British sociologist Ruth Towse (1995): artists on average earned below poverty levels
- Digital tools eliminated that false binary

**Tactics for cultivating true fans:**
- Maintain direct relationship — no intermediaries taking majority cut
- Crowdfunding: Kickstarter has raised $2.5B+ across 100,000+ projects
- Tiered patronage: Jill Sobule raised $50,000 for album recording; tiers from $10 downloads to $10,000 "sing on the CD" credit
- Pre-sell (Street Performer Protocol): announce "I'll release the novel when $100K in donations arrive"; readers see live progress bar
- Patreon model: fund ongoing projects — monthly magazine, video series, artist salary
- "Whatever your interests as a creator are, your 1,000 true fans are one click from you"

**Corporate disadvantage:** "Big corporations are institutionally unable to find and deliver niche audiences." The niche is yours alone.

**Time caveat:** "Cultivating a thousand true fans is time consuming, sometimes nerve-racking, and not for everyone." If you don't want to deal with fans, hire that function out. But then you're back to giving up the majority.

**Key quotes:**
- "To be a successful creator you don't need millions. You don't need millions of dollars or millions of customers, millions of clients or millions of fans."
- "Whatever your interests as a creator are, your 1,000 true fans are one click from you."
- "You don't need to impress the president; you need to impress your fans."

**Why in guide:** The antidote to vanity metrics. Reframes X from "build a big audience" to "cultivate the exact 1K who will pre-buy Lancelot and seed skale-brain." WHO follows you beats HOW MANY.

## Build Your Spiky Point of View to Stand Out — https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=1Tnsijy5km0 — transcribed — STRONG

**Thesis (Wes Kao, Maven co-founder, 39-min talk):** In a noisy world where thousands of practitioners share similar backgrounds, a spiky point of view is the asymmetric distribution weapon. It's the tactical drill-down on how to build one.

**Exact definition:** "A spiky point of view is your unique perspective on the topic of your expertise that other people — other experts — might disagree with. If you brought 20 marketers into a room and you said your spiky point of view, 19 other people might disagree with it, and they would have good reason."

The key: disagreement must be *grounded in evidence and logic*, not just contrarianism. Both sides can have rational arguments.

**Why it matters for distribution:**
- Thousands of people with "similar backgrounds, similar years of experience, similar offerings" — if you say the same things, customers/students have no rational reason to pick you
- Safe takes die in the feed; spiky takes attract your exact people
- Puts out "the bat signal — everyone could see it" — people who believe what you believe come out of the woodwork feeling "finally someone said this"
- "You attract like my people" — creates specific Affinity that generic content never does

**Concrete Maven example:**
- Wes's spiky POV: "If you want to create a course, you have to think about demand first — the marketing first — before you even build curriculum, worksheets, or slides"
- Most people do the opposite: build first, market after
- Maven itself was launched before it had a name, a technical co-founder, or a built product — validated demand first

**How to draw out your own spiky POV (questions to ask):**
1. What do you believe that you wish other people understood?
2. What do other people say that makes you want to pull your hair out — where you totally disagree?
3. What are you almost afraid to say because you know people will disagree?
4. What have you seen from years of direct experience that contradicts common advice?

**Outside-in / Inside-out framework (for course/content topics):**
- **Outside-in**: market demand — how many people want this, how urgent is the problem, do employers reimburse it? What questions do people already come to you for?
- **Inside-out**: what can you talk about for years without burning out, what do you see with fresh eyes always?
- Weight: outside-in circle should be significantly bigger. "You really don't want to spend effort building something and launch to silence, crickets, tumbleweed."

**Compounding via spiky POV:**
- "Impossible to imitate — rooted in your conviction and authenticity"
- When you find a good one: "people's eyes light up and they want to hear more"
- Use it across entire funnel: LinkedIn posts → email → landing page positioning
- "April McLean teaches community building — when you think of community you usually think of... [but her spiky take reframes the whole category]"

**Key quotes:**
- "We live in a really noisy world... if you don't offer anything new, it's really hard for customers to justify why to pick you"
- "When people come out of the woodwork and you realize a bunch of people thought this too but were also afraid to say it — all those people feel Affinity toward you"
- "A spiky point of view is almost impossible to imitate because it's rooted in your conviction and authenticity"

**Why in guide:** The how-to implementation under the essay. Specificity, grounded-in-expertise, not-just-contrarianism — exactly the mechanics needed to translate benchmark numbers and architecture opinions into a defensible content stance.

## How I Built an $8M Business with 0 Employees — https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ZkwYxYfkfYU — transcribed — STRONG

**Thesis (Justin Welsh, Creator MBA founder, 43-min talk):** Solo solopreneurship compounds when you build a specific audience + a stacked product ladder + a time-allocation system. Every piece reinforces the next.

**The product ladder (specificity-first model):**
- Start with a $50 product: "If they spend $50, can you deliver $50,000 of value and set the stage for a deep future relationship for the next decade?"
- As audience grows → core mid-tier product (~$200-500)
- At scale (1M+ followers, same specific topic for years) → high-ticket offer (e.g., Creator MBA)
- Justin: "I've been beating the same drum for so long — that's why the high-ticket offer works when I finally drop it"
- Reverse failure mode: generic audience (productivity in general) → can't productize effectively as you scale; "be the guy who solves a very specific problem for a very specific person"

**Audience as a validation machine:**
- Before building, find 5-10 people with the exact problem; are they willing to spend money AND time?
- "If nobody's willing to spend money and nobody's willing to spend time, you have an idea but not a business"
- One-on-one → extract commonalities → those become a framework → that framework becomes a productized service → then a course
- "This is exactly how I started my business"

**Niche discovery — four methods:**
1. **Practical experience/credibility**: what have you done at work for 10+ years? Justin: sales VP for 16 years → healthcare startup consulting
2. **Learned obsession**: something you're obsessed with but never did professionally → you became an expert anyway through massive consumption
3. **What people come to you for**: family and friends routing specific questions → pay attention to this
4. **Problems you've solved for yourself**: if you're solving it, how many others have it and would pay?

**The enemy/movement marketing tactic:**
- "Marketing is a lot like politics — rarely about what you do, mostly about what the enemy does wrong"
- Pick a target: an ideology, a mantra, a movement you're 100% against (doesn't have to be a person)
- "Throw rocks at your enemies" (Blair Warren quote) — builds a movement because movements form by defeating an enemy
- Justin's enemy: the idea that career advancement = leading more people. His movement: individual contributor excellence, solopreneurship

**Time allocation system (quarterly):**
- List every task you do in a week with time estimates
- Score each task 1-10: how much does this drive my intended outcome?
- Calculate ratio: (score) / (hours per week) = value per hour
- Keep top 20%, eliminate/automate/delegate/simplify the rest
- Quarterly refresh because life changes

**Path to solopreneurship (phase approach):**
- Don't quit cold; build while employed
- 15 minutes/day practicing marketing yourself is a good start
- "Once you reach 60-70% of your take-home from the side, you're likely to continue to grow"
- "Timing matters — I started LinkedIn in 2018 before it was popular. Don't follow my path and hope for the same luck. Follow the gradual phase approach."

**Burnout origin (non-obvious):** Burnout comes from loss of control, not hard work. "If you've played Tetris — as levels get harder, pieces fall faster, one mistake compounds into 16. That's what losing control at a company feels like." Panic attack December 2018 → the inflection point.

**Key quotes:**
- "Be the guy who solves a very specific problem that's painful for a very specific company or person"
- "My first product was $50 — if they spend $50, can you deliver $50,000 of value?"
- "So many people are smart, have great products, but can't communicate. Communicating your value is the thing that matters most."
- "Just because you see someone who did it faster doesn't mean that's the right way to do it"
- "I'm not the Creator MBA — I'm the person who teaches solopreneurship. Everything I make is about that one specific thing."

**Why in guide:** Sharpest operator playbook for turning an X audience into a product flywheel: stacked offers, validate through the audience before building, time-allocation system to stay on the 20%. Directly applicable to building Lancelot + skale-brain as a one-person product stack.

## Spiky Point of View: Let's Get Controversial — https://www.weskao.com/blog/spiky-point-of-view-lets-get-a-little-controversial — read — STRONG

**Thesis:** A spiky POV is an opinionated, defensible stance grounded in real expertise. It generates legitimate disagreement, not just outrage. It is the key mechanism by which founders with real knowledge separate from noise.

**Five-element framework:**
1. **Debatable** — must allow disagreement; universal agreement means not spiky enough
2. **Non-gratuitous** — controversy from genuine conviction, not manufactured outrage
3. **Educates audience** — "makes people see their problem in a new way"
4. **Evidence-based** — defensible without requiring consensus agreement
5. **Conviction-driven** — you advocate actively, not passively present

**Spiky vs. generic (worked examples):**
- Generic: "Teach as you learn"
- Spiky: "If you don't have it all figured out — this is a feature, not a bug. Two ways to build community: (a) be an expert, (b) be a beginner. Most people wrongly assume expertise is the prerequisite."

Other spiky POVs Kao holds:
- "Course builders must embed marketing from day one — not after the curriculum is built"
- "Online instruction is 50% teaching, 50% entertainment"
- "Scenario planning (30 minutes) beats prototyping sprints (5 days)"
- "'Start with why' has enabled professional navel-gazing instead of customer focus"
- "Experiments carry hidden costs — only run one if results will change your future behavior"

**How to develop yours:**
1. Identify 3-5 things you believe that others in your field don't
2. Surface your frustrations — emotion + conviction = potential spiky take
3. Note moments when someone said "I've never thought of it that way"
4. Question accepted practices that underperform in your direct experience
5. Refine through conversation (X, customer calls, peers)
6. Expect initial genericness; iteration sharpens it

**Critical warnings:**
- Never copy others' SPOVs — "your energy and conviction won't be the same"
- Fear is the main blocker: "What's preventing you from coming up with spiky points of view? Fear."
- Random opinions without credibility don't work — must demonstrate relevant track record

**Key quotes:**
- "A spiky POV is a perspective others can disagree with. It's a belief you feel strongly about and are willing to advocate for."
- "The goal is to share your truth and start a conversation — not to aim for 100% agreement."
- "You'll know you have a great spiky point of view when your audience's eyes light up and they want to hear more."
- "When you parrot someone else, your energy level and sense of conviction won't be the same."

**Why in guide:** The single tactic that separates compounding founder accounts from stagnant ones. Benchmark numbers and architecture opinions are raw material — spiky POV construction is the transformation step.

## Status as a Service — https://www.eugenewei.com/blog/2019/2/19/status-as-a-service — read — STRONG

**Thesis:** Social networks are status games. Every successful one creates a new form of social capital (a "token") earned through a specific proof of work. Understanding this mechanism explains why certain content earns disproportionate social capital and why platforms rise and fall.

1. "People are status-seeking monkeys"
2. People pursue "the most efficient path to maximizing social capital"

**The StaaS framework:**
Every successful platform: (1) issues a new form of social capital, (2) requires specific proof of work to earn it, (3) becomes harder to mine over time (built-in scarcity). Early movers accumulate disproportionate capital.

**Proof of work by platform:**
- Facebook (early): witty text + Harvard email requirement
- Instagram: interesting square photo composition
- Vine: entertaining 6-second video
- Twitter: amusing text ≤140 characters
- TikTok: choreographed lip-sync routines

**Why proof of work matters:** "If everyone can achieve a certain type of status, it's no status at all — it's a participation trophy." **The cryptocurrency analogy:**
Each platform issues a token. Users show proof of work to earn it. Over time it becomes harder to mine, creating scarcity. People mock both until they don't.

**Facebook News Feed as "greatest social capital creation event in tech history":**
- Before: you browsed individual profiles
- After: everyone's content merged into a live, updating scoreboard
- "Suddenly be dropped into a talent show competing against every person you had ever met"
- Triggered explosive behavioral adaptation as users discovered optimal content strategies

**Why youth dominate new status games:**
1. Capital poverty — lack job titles, assets, credentials; virtual status is the fastest path
2. Adults already possess prestige; they optimize maintaining it, not mining new networks
3. Youth have temporal surplus — low hurdle rate for experimental platform exploration
4. Identity multiplicity — teens maintain distinct social personas (school/home/friends)
5. Neural plasticity — faster at learning new status game mechanics

**Three network arcs:**
1. "Come for the tool, stay for the network" (Instagram: started with filters, stayed for photo composition as status)
2. "Come for the fame, stay for the tool" (Wikipedia, Quora: users contributed for status, platforms accumulated valuable datasets)

**Evaporative cooling — why networks die:**

**How platforms extend shelf life:**
- Repeated proof of work innovation: Instagram added aspect ratios, video, Stories, Reels

- New proof of work = new competition = new status to earn
